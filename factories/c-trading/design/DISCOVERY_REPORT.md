# Honest gates, not clever search, find winners

Published research and practitioner experience agree on one point above all others: a strategy factory earns trust from its evaluation gate, not from its idea generator. Ideas are cheap, and an automated search over thousands of variants will always produce attractive backtests; in the best 2026 audit, an honest evaluation stack (leak-proof tools, a ledger of every trial, deflated Sharpe, PBO, held-out confidence intervals) certified passive benchmarks and **rejected every LLM-discovered strategy** ([arXiv 2608.27734](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.27734)). The edges with the longest out-of-sample record are slow, diversified, economically explainable premia (trend following, carry, value plus momentum), and even those deliver live Sharpe ratios far below their backtests: the largest trend funds have earned a **Sharpe of about 0.30 since 2000** ([AQR](https://www.aqr.com/-/media/AQR/Documents/Alternative-Thinking/AQR-Alternative-Thinking---Hold-the-Dip.pdf?sc_lang=en)). A "stated level of confidence" is achievable, but only as a statement about the search process (trial-adjusted significance, probability of overfitting, a single-use holdout), because paper trading of a few months cannot statistically confirm a strategy with a Sharpe near 1; that takes roughly three years or more. Paper trading's honest role is to catch implementation, cost and data errors, to check that live results sit inside the backtest's predicted range, and to accumulate evidence across the whole portfolio of strategies. From Australia, a free stack exists (vectorbt for mining, an event-driven engine for confirmation, Alpaca, OANDA and Binance or OKX for paper trading, Binance public data for crypto), but free equity data is not survivorship-free and crypto derivatives access is restricted. The findings point to a factory whose main product is correct rejections, and they leave open several choices the next design phase must settle: universe, confidence definition, trial budget, and how much human review the gate needs.

## Quant firms treat ideas as cheap and gatekeeping as the product

### An assembly line with a gatekeeper who is not the idea's author

Respected practitioners start from an economic reason an edge should exist (a risk premium, a behavioural bias, or a structural flow such as hedgers paying speculators) and only then test a small, pre-constrained family of rules. Rob Carver calls this "ideas first" and shows how to shrink a moving-average crossover's 65,280 possible parameter pairs to a handful by fixing ratios, using a doubling grid of lookbacks and sharing parameters across instruments ([Carver](https://qoppac.blogspot.com/2015/11/the-three-kinds-of-overfitting.html)). Citadel's equity quant group says the same thing from the institutional side: "ideas become cheap ... What really matters is executing and prioritizing among them", with a process that states the mechanism and how to disprove it, measures consistency, simulates with realistic costs, and uses held-out data "only near the end of the process" ([Citadel](https://www.citadel.com/careers/career-perspectives/inside-eqr-alpha-research)).

Firms organise this as a production line. López de Prado's "meta-strategy paradigm" has separate stations for data curation, feature analysis, strategy design, backtesting, deployment and portfolio oversight; the strategist must name "the economic mechanism that causes an agent to lose money to us", and the backtester must evaluate overfitting "taking into account the number of trials it took" ([AFML ch.1](https://catalogimages.wiley.com/images/db/pdf/9781119482086.excerpt.pdf)). Centralised shops add peer review: at Winton, an improvement needed "a peer group review of some rigour" and validation by someone else before integration ([Hedge Fund Journal](https://thehedgefundjournal.com/winton-capital-management)). The common thread is separation of roles: the person who generates an idea never approves it.

WorldQuant's BRAIN platform is the closest public template for a mechanical gate. Mirrored copies of its documentation list the user-tier tests: **Sharpe above 1.25, fitness above 1.0, turnover between 1% and 70%, no stock above 10% weight, a sub-universe test, and PnL correlation below 0.7 with existing alphas** unless the Sharpe is at least 10% higher ([BRAIN docs mirror](http://61.182.4.91:8765/wiki/view?path=raw%2Farticles%2Fworldquant-brain-learn-docs%2Fraw%2Fclear-these-tests-before-submitting-an-alpha.md)). These thresholds come from mirrors, since the original blocks automated access, so treat the numbers as indicative. WorldQuant's own book adds a rule that matters for any automated search: judge the whole batch. If 100 machine-generated alphas average an out-of-sample information ratio of 0.01, reject all of them, "including the 60 with positive out-of-sample performance" ([Finding Alphas](https://jchenmir.github.io/download/Finding_Alphas__A_Quantitative_Approach_to_Building_Trading_Strategies-Igor_Tulchinsky_et_al.pdf)).

Record keeping is part of the method, not administration. Carver advises recording every backtest and keeping a "fund level" backtest holding every idea ever considered; in his example, quietly dropping unprofitable variants inflated a backtest Sharpe from 0.17 to 0.27 ([Carver](https://qoppac.blogspot.com/2015/11/the-three-kinds-of-overfitting.html?m=0)).

### Combine many weak signals; do not crown a champion

Backtest rank is a poor selector. Across 888 Quantopian algorithms with at least six months of live data, backtest Sharpe had **R² below 0.025** for out-of-sample performance, and the more backtests an author ran, the larger the shortfall ([Wiecki et al.](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=2745220)). Firms therefore combine. WorldQuant's 101 published production alphas have an average pairwise correlation of only 15.9% ([Kakushadze et al.](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/wilm.10525)). In Carver's tests, equal-weighting the rules that pass a cost check gave Sharpe 0.726 and beat every fitted weighting scheme (0.545 to 0.656) ([Carver](https://qoppac.blogspot.com/2021/05/fit-forecast-weights-by-instrument-by.html)). His cost check, the "speed limit", is to spend no more than one third of expected pre-cost returns on costs ([Carver](https://qoppac.blogspot.com/2020/04/how-fast-should-we-trade.html?m=1)). Combination carries its own trap: picking the best k of n candidate signals produces a bias almost as large as picking the single best of n^k ([Novy-Marx](https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w21329/w21329.pdf)), so the selection of ensemble members must be counted as trials too.

### Slow adaptation, gradual allocation, pre-committed retirement

López de Prado describes the lifecycle after the backtest as embargo (run on data after the backtest ends), paper trading "for as long as it is needed to gather enough evidence", graduation to a small allocation, automatic re-allocation on a concave curve that starts small, grows as the strategy performs as expected and shrinks as it decays, and finally decommissioning when underperformance lasts long enough "to conclude that the supporting theory is no longer backed by empirical evidence" ([AFML ch.1](https://catalogimages.wiley.com/images/db/pdf/9781119482086.excerpt.pdf)). No firm publishes how long incubation lasts or what refit cadence it uses; Carver is the only detailed public source.

Adaptation is deliberately slow. Carver refits on an expanding window for slow effects, never tests parameters on data that preceded the fit ("no time machine"), and warns against "improving" a system whose performance is acceptable ([Carver](https://qoppac.blogspot.com/2015/11/the-three-kinds-of-overfitting.html?m=0)). AQR found adding factor timing "extremely challenging" and concludes that disciplined exposure to a diversified portfolio "is hard to beat" ([AQR](https://www.aqr.com/-/media/AQR/Documents/Journal-Articles/AQRJPMQuant23FactFictionandFactorInvesting.pdf?sc_lang=en)). Two Sigma publishes a four-state regime model, but as a tool for understanding portfolios, not a trading signal ([Two Sigma](https://www.twosigma.com/articles/a-machine-learning-approach-to-regime-modeling)), and recent academic tests find hidden-Markov regime allocation "best interpreted as risk-management tools rather than universal return-enhancing solutions" ([ScienceDirect 2026](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S105905602600479X)).

Decay is real and measurable at the population level. Across 97 published equity predictors, returns were **26% lower out of sample and 58% lower after publication**, with larger decay for predictors with higher in-sample returns and those built only from price and volume ([McLean & Pontiff](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1111/jofi.12365)). For 215 bank-marketed alternative-beta strategies, median Sharpe fell from **1.20 in backtest to 0.31 live** ([Suhonen et al.](https://community.portfolio123.com/uploads/short-url/eDD8GQ0ZmwCF8vCR4dORON040Sf.pdf)). Detecting decay in a single strategy is much harder. CFM's research shows a ten-year process with a true Sharpe of 0.5 has a 5% chance of a drawdown lasting seven years or more ([CFM](https://www.cfm.com/wp-content/uploads/2022/12/317-2017-You-are-in-a-drawdown-When-should-you-start-worrying.pdf)). One blog simulation found that common kill rules halved a healthy Sharpe-1.0 strategy 81% of the time within three years while letting a zero-edge strategy run unpaused through its first year 82% of the time ([Backtesting Arena](https://tradingstrategies.work/blog/trading-kill-rules-false-alarms)); this is an unreviewed source, but it matches the CFM arithmetic. Practitioners recommend writing kill rules before deployment and calibrating them to the strategy's own out-of-sample distribution, for example a Monte Carlo envelope for the live equity curve ([Kinlay](https://jonathankinlay.com/2018/11/strategy-still-working)).

## Slow, diversified premia hold the evidence; fast edges are closed

The families that survived out of sample are risk premia or slow behavioural effects that carry crash risk. The families that died were pure mispricings that became easy to arbitrage once costs fell. That pattern is the most useful prior a factory can hold.

Trend following has the deepest record: positive in every decade from 1880 to 2016 across 67 markets, with an average per-market Sharpe near 0.4 ([Hurst, Ooi & Pedersen](https://www.aqr.com/-/media/AQR/Documents/Insights/Journal-Article/AQR-JPM-Fall-2017.pdf)). The value comes from breadth: the median single-asset Sharpe is only 0.34 to 0.39, but the diversified combination reaches a gross 1.60 ([Babu et al.](https://images.aqr.com/-/media/AQR/Documents/Insights/Journal-Article/AQR-Trends-Everywhere_JOIM.pdf)). Speed matters: in Carver's backtests, crossovers faster than about 8/32 days lose money after costs ([Carver](https://qoppac.blogspot.com/2020/04/how-fast-should-we-trade.html)). The live record is sobering. The SG Trend index returned +27.3% in 2022 ([Hedgeweek](https://www.advancedalphaadvisers.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/11/HW-Trend-Following-The-Outlook-for-CTA-and-Systematic-Investing.pdf)), yet CTAs were the worst hedge fund strategy in the first half of 2025 after tariff-driven reversals ([With Intelligence](https://www.withintelligence.com/insights/cta-hedge-fund-report)). Carry is the other strong premium: Sharpe about 0.8 within an asset class and 1.2 diversified, with large losses in global recessions ([Koijen et al.](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0304405X17302908)). Combining styles is where the evidence is strongest: value, momentum, carry and defensive each have century-long Sharpe ratios of 0.53 to 0.68 across asset classes, and **the four-style combination reaches 1.46** ([Ilmanen et al.](https://images.aqr.com/-/media/AQR/Documents/Journal-Articles/JOIM_How-Do-Factor-Premia-Vary-Over-Time.pdf)).

Two consequences follow. First, standalone Sharpe is the wrong yardstick. Carver keeps mean-reversion rules with a negative standalone Sharpe (−0.63) because they offset trend ([Carver](https://qoppac.blogspot.com/2021/12/my-trading-system.html)), and his rule of thumb shows a carry-plus-trend system rising from Sharpe 0.35 on one instrument to 0.70 on 37 ([Carver](https://qoppac.blogspot.com/2016/03/diversification-and-small-account-size.html)). Second, the strategies a naive miner finds most easily, short-horizon price and volume patterns, are the worst category: 96% of "trading frictions" anomalies fail to replicate ([Hou, Xue & Zhang](https://academic.oup.com/rfs/article-abstract/33/5/2019/5236964)). Carver is blunt about the intraday end of the spectrum: profitable day trading at retail commissions and spreads "is an idea that deserves to be rebutted constantly" ([Carver](https://qoppac.blogspot.com/2019/10/new-book-leveraged-trading.html)).

Crypto deserves a caution of its own. Time-series momentum shows up under realistic costs but is concentrated in bull markets, and coins move with Bitcoin, which limits diversification ([AUT/ACFR](https://acfr.aut.ac.nz/__data/assets/pdf_file/0009/918729/Time_Series_and_Cross_Sectional_Momentum_in_the_Cryptocurrency_Market_with_IA.pdf)); large-cap cross-sectional crypto momentum turned negative and insignificant after July 2020 ([Grobys et al.](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11408-025-00474-9)). The notes found no sourced live record of trend or carry implemented on ETFs only, and no evidence on anomaly persistence in ASX equities. Both are gaps for an Australian operator.

## Realistic backtests charge the spread, lag every fill and distrust free data

Explicit fees are now the small part of trading costs outside crypto. Alpaca charges no commission on US stocks and ETFs ([Alpaca fee schedule](https://files.alpaca.markets/disclosures/library/BrokFeeSched.pdf)); an Australian account at Interactive Brokers pays Pro rates of $0.0035 a share because the free Lite tier is for US residents ([IBKR](https://www.interactivebrokers.com/en/pricing/commissions-stocks.php)). Binance charges 0.10% a side on spot and 0.02% maker / 0.05% taker on USD-margined futures at the entry tier ([Binance spot](https://www.binance.com/en/fee/trading), [Binance futures](https://www.binance.com/en/fee/futureFee)), and perpetual positions pay or receive funding, with a default interest component of 0.01% per eight hours ([Binance FAQ](https://www.binance.com/en/support/faq/detail/360033525031)). Fees change often and the notes could not verify Binance Australia's schedule, so these figures need re-checking before they are hard-coded.

The implicit costs dominate. Novy-Marx and Velikov estimate round-trip costs above 50 basis points for typical anomaly strategies and find that very few strategies with more than 50% monthly turnover keep a significant net return ([Novy-Marx & Velikov](https://mysimon.rochester.edu/novy-marx/research/ToAatTC.pdf)). AQR's live institutional trades cost about a tenth of that, but AQR itself says the higher estimates describe "a small liquidity demander" ([Frazzini, Israel & Moskowitz](https://spinup-000d1a-wp-offload-media.s3.amazonaws.com/faculty/wp-content/uploads/sites/3/2019/09/TradingCostofAssetPricingAnomalies.pdf)), which is what a retail bot sending market orders is. After publication and costs, the average equal-weighted anomaly portfolio nets **−3 basis points a month** ([Chen & Velikov](https://jacobslevycenter.wharton.upenn.edu/wp-content/uploads/2019/09/Accounting-for-the-Anomaly-Zoo.pdf)). For size-dependent impact, commercial models share one form: spread cost plus a constant of order one times daily volatility times the square root of order size over daily volume ([Gatheral](https://mfe.baruch.cuny.edu/wp-content/uploads/2012/09/Chicago2016OptimalExecution.pdf)). QuantConnect's LEAN engine offers working templates, including a volume-share slippage model that caps fills at 2.5% of bar volume by default ([QuantConnect](https://www.quantconnect.com/docs/v2/writing-algorithms/reality-modeling/slippage/supported-models)). The notes found no authoritative standard for stress-testing at two or three times estimated costs; it is common practice, and the pass thresholds are a design choice.

The biases that make backtests lie fall into two groups. The first is mechanical. Yahoo data generally lacks delisted stocks, and a backtest on current S&P 500 members "easily beat" the equal-weight index until point-in-time constituents were used, after which it matched ([Koker](https://teddykoker.com/2019/05/creating-a-survivorship-bias-free-sp-500-dataset-with-python/)). The yfinance library returns dividend-adjusted prices by default, so a "Close" column can silently be a total-return series ([yfinance issue](https://github.com/ranaroussi/yfinance/issues/1749)). Binance spot files switched to microsecond timestamps from January 2025 while futures examples still use milliseconds ([binance-public-data](https://github.com/binance/binance-public-data)), a ready-made alignment bug. Limit orders are another: a backtest that fills whenever price touches the limit overstates fills, and Alpaca's paper engine will not expose the error because it fills once the quote crosses and models no queue ([Alpaca](https://docs.alpaca.markets/us/docs/paper-trading)). The standard defences (execute no earlier than the bar after the signal, lag fundamentals to their release date, use point-in-time universes, fit scalers on the training window only) are established practice, though the notes did not source a measurement of how much each bias inflates returns.

The second group is statistical and grows with every strategy tested: data snooping. That is the subject of the next section. One ordering rule connects the two groups: backtest correctness must be established before any overfitting statistic is computed, because the statistics cannot see a leak. A deliberately leaky strategy in the 2026 audit reached a Sharpe of 34.7 and passed deflated Sharpe and PBO "completely" ([arXiv 2608.27734](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.27734v1)).

Every candidate also needs a like-for-like benchmark after costs: cash, buy-and-hold of the traded asset, and a simple passive mix. Forum experience is thin in the notes (Reddit could not be read), but the one Hacker News thread retrieved repeats a consistent story of bull-market gains mistaken for skill, including crypto traders who measured returns in dollars and lagged simply holding Bitcoin ([Hacker News](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=34766728)).

## Confidence comes from counting trials; paper trading confirms plumbing

### No Sharpe threshold is safe without the trial count

The False Strategy Theorem states the problem: "With enough number of trials, any false strategy may achieve a Sharpe ratio as high as a researcher demands" ([López de Prado](https://quantresearch.org/Publications.htm)). With only five years of daily data, trying 45 independent variations makes a best Sharpe of 1.0 likely with no skill at all ([Bailey et al.](https://sdm.lbl.gov/oapapers/ssrn-id2507040-bailey.pdf)). The authors also say the popular habit of halving a backtest Sharpe "has no scientific basis" ([Bailey & López de Prado](https://academic.oup.com/jrssig/article/18/6/22/7038278)). Empirical haircuts of about half are a fair description of what happened to published anomalies, but they are not a substitute for counting trials.

The tools divide by what they test. The deflated Sharpe ratio (DSR) is the probability that a strategy's true Sharpe exceeds the best Sharpe expected from N zero-skill trials, adjusted for sample length, skew and fat tails; it needs the number of trials and the variance of Sharpe across them ([Bailey & López de Prado](https://www.davidhbailey.com/dhbpapers/deflated-sharpe.pdf)). N should be the effective number of independent trials, which López de Prado estimates by clustering the correlation matrix of trial returns; in his example 6,385 trials collapsed to four clusters because the search was constrained by theory, while arbitrary searches would yield "perhaps in the hundreds" ([López de Prado 2019](https://www.aqr.com/-/media/AQR/Documents/Journal-Articles/JFDS_Winter2019_A-Data-Science-Solution-to-Multiple-Testing-Crisis---Lopez_de_Prado.pdf?sc_lang=en)). A secondary worked example gives the scale: a best-of-50 Sharpe of 1.0 on five years of daily data has a DSR near 0.5, a coin flip, and about 1.74 is needed for a DSR of 0.95 ([Risk Hub](https://riskhub.org/blogs/multiple-comparisons-in-backtesting)).

The probability of backtest overfitting (PBO) tests the search, not the strategy. It splits the matrix of every trial's returns into 16 blocks, forms every half-and-half combination, and measures how often the in-sample winner ranks below the median out of sample. The authors set conditions that bind a factory directly: all trials must be reported, the test does not check that the backtest is correct, and it "should not be applied to guide the search" ([Bailey et al.](https://www.davidhbailey.com/dhbpapers/backtest-prob.pdf)). The notes found no authoritative PBO threshold for a go or no-go decision.

For comparing many correlated candidates against a benchmark, bootstrap tests keep the dependence between strategies that Bonferroni-style corrections ignore: White's Reality Check, Hansen's more powerful SPA test, and the Romano-Wolf stepwise method that identifies every strategy that beats the benchmark ([Romano & Wolf](https://econ-papers.upf.edu/papers/712.pdf)). Harvey, Liu and Zhu's analytic route yields the well-known hurdle that a new factor should clear **a t-statistic above 3.0**, which the authors consider probably too low ([Harvey, Liu & Zhu](https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w20592/w20592.pdf)). A dissenting view holds that most published findings are likely true and the dispute is interpretive ([Chen](https://arxiv.org/html/2206.15365v10)); it does not change the arithmetic for a search the factory itself runs.

### Walk-forward shows one path; CPCV shows a distribution

Walk-forward analysis tests a single historical path. Combinatorial purged cross-validation (CPCV) removes training observations whose labels overlap the test set, adds an embargo buffer, and recombines folds into many out-of-sample paths. In a controlled synthetic study it beat k-fold, purged k-fold "and especially Walk-Forward" on PBO and DSR ([Arian, Norouzi & Seco](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=4778909)). The evidence is one synthetic study, and practitioners object that CPCV trains on data that comes after the test period. Carver's alternative is an expanding-window fit pooled across instruments, and he distrusts short windows: "A year is a very, very short period of time to actually find any sort of statistical evidence" ([Better System Trader](https://bettersystemtrader.com/026-robert-carver/)). The notes' judgement is that CPCV matters for strategies with a fitted step, while fixed-rule strategies are adequately covered by sub-period splits plus PBO across the parameter grid.

Holdout discipline is where automation helps most. Timothy Masters puts the rule plainly: "Every time you look at the out-of-sample results and decide to keep or discard a variant, you have used that data" ([Better System Trader](https://bettersystemtrader.com/185-how-to-avoid-creating-trading-strategies-that-degrade-quickly-timothy-masters/)). A holdout is therefore single-use per candidate family. Masters' alternative, a Monte Carlo permutation test that shuffles returns and re-runs the entire search on each shuffled series, gives the most honest null for "best of search", at roughly a thousand times the search cost.

Trial budgets follow from the minimum backtest length, approximately 2·ln(N)/SR² years. For a target Sharpe of 1.0, ten trials justify about 2.5 years of data, a hundred about 6.4, and a thousand about 10.6 ([Quant Memo](https://quantmemo.com/concepts/minimum-backtest-length)). The implication is uncomfortable for a perpetual miner: the cumulative trial count on a fixed dataset only grows, so either the hurdle rises for ever or the factory must find fresh data (new markets, new time).

Robustness checks beyond these (parameter plateaus, noise injection, tests on other markets) are practitioner craft. Vendors such as StrategyQuant order them as a funnel from cheap to expensive ([StrategyQuant](https://strategyquant.com/doc/cross-checks-robust-testing-overview)), which is sound engineering, but the notes found no primary study measuring how well they work, and they do not count trials.

### What paper trading can and cannot prove

The t-statistic of a Sharpe ratio grows with the square root of time, so the record needed for 95% confidence is about (1.96/SR)² years ([Papers With Backtest](https://paperswithbacktest.com/strategies/systematic-trading-strategies)). A secondary worked example puts a Sharpe-1.0 strategy at **3.7 years** against a zero hurdle with realistic skew and kurtosis, and about 14.5 years against a 0.5 hurdle ([portfolio-lab](https://portfolio-lab.net/knowledge/deflated-sharpe-ratio)). Carver's figures are starker for realistic single-instrument rules: at Sharpe 0.5 about ten years, at 0.3 nearly forty ([7 Circles](https://the7circles.uk/systematic-trading-2-fitting-and-allocation)). Kevin Davey incubates strategies for 6 to 12 months before risking money ([AlgoAdvantage](https://algoadvantage.substack.com/p/036-kevin-davey-part-i-its-all-about)), a period that cannot confirm skill at these Sharpe levels.

Paper engines are also optimistic. Alpaca's does not model market impact, latency, queue position, regulatory fees or dividends, and does not check order size against quoted size ([Alpaca](https://docs.alpaca.markets/us/docs/paper-trading)). Interactive Brokers simulates fills from the top of the book only ([IBKR](https://www.ibkrguides.com/brokerportal/aboutpapertradingaccounts.htm)). Paper profit is an upper bound.

What paper trading honestly provides is fourfold. It verifies that the live implementation matches the backtest, the check López de Prado assigns to the deployment team. It measures the gap between expected and simulated fills. It tests consistency, meaning whether the live result falls inside the range the backtest predicted. And across many low-correlation strategies it builds portfolio-level evidence faster than any single record, in the spirit of WorldQuant's comparison of in-sample and out-of-sample results across everything a designer produces. The last three roles are the notes' synthesis; no primary study sets an optimal incubation length or a sequential-testing design.

## A free stack exists from Australia, with two real gaps

For mining, the notes recommend a vectorised engine, then a re-run of survivors in an event-driven engine that shares code with paper trading. Release dates below were pulled from PyPI on 17 November 2026.

QuantConnect's paper trading needs a paid live node ([QuantConnect](https://www.quantconnect.com/docs/v2/cloud-platform/live-trading/brokerages/quantconnect-paper-trading)).

On data, Binance's public archive is the best free source: all symbols, klines from one second to one month, trades and futures, under a non-commercial licence ([binance-public-data](https://github.com/binance/binance-public-data)). Alpaca's free tier gives US bars since 2016 at 200 calls a minute ([Alpaca](https://docs.alpaca.markets/us/docs/about-market-data-api)), and Stooq offers free bulk daily, hourly and five-minute files ([Stooq](https://stooq.com/db/h)). yfinance is unofficial and rate-limited, and delisted tickers vanish from it ([fruitstand.dev](https://fruitstand.dev/blog/is-yfinance-still-usable)), so it suits ad hoc checks, not a pipeline.

The two gaps are these. First, no free source reliably provides delisted stocks and historical index membership; the cheapest sourced fix is Norgate's Platinum tier at US$630 a year ([waylandz](https://waylandz.com/quant-book-en/Data-Provider-Comparison)). Until then, single-stock cross-sectional strategies cannot be trusted, and the universe is effectively ETFs, FX and crypto. Second, crypto derivatives: Binance Australia has been spot-only since ASIC cancelled its derivatives licence in April 2023 ([OzCoinTrail](https://ozcointrail.com/exchanges/is-binance-legal-in-australia)), and whether an Australian user can reach Binance's futures demo is unverified. Funding and basis carry, one of the better-evidenced ideas, is therefore easy to backtest on free data and hard to paper-trade from Australia. ASX data sources were not researched.

Alpaca also publishes an official MCP server that defaults to paper trading and can be restricted to chosen toolsets ([Alpaca MCP](https://github.com/alpacahq/alpaca-mcp-server)), a low-risk way for an agent to inspect paper results.

## LLM agents build the factory well and find edge poorly

Published LLM mining systems have converged on one loop: hypothesis, code or formula, backtest, structured feedback, and a memory of past attempts. Microsoft's RD-Agent(Q) splits research from development and uses a bandit scheduler to choose the next direction ([arXiv 2505.15155](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.15155)). AlphaAgent adds three regularisers: originality measured by syntax-tree overlap with existing alphas, alignment between hypothesis and formula, and complexity limits ([arXiv 2502.16789](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.16789)). Man Group runs a production version with an idea agent, an implementer and an evaluator, where every signal must "pass identical evaluation thresholds" to human research and the firm concedes "we can't leave it unsupervised just yet" ([Man Group](https://www.man.com/insights/what-ai-can-do-for-alpha)).

The reported results do not survive independent scrutiny well. Rankings disagree across papers: RD-Agent's annualised return is 0.78% in AlphaAgent's paper, about 14% in its own, and 5.99% in an independent re-benchmark where the best LLM method (7.22%) barely beat a hand-built factor library (6.32%) ([CosmosAlpha](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.04555v1)). Most benchmarks are Chinese equities over periods that overlap the models' training data. For LLMs that write complete strategies, the audits are harsher. Across 403 strategy-asset audits of 198 strategies from five models, the mean delivered Sharpe was −0.09, **92% failed to beat buy-and-hold**, and every apparent improvement was explained by net long exposure, not timing ([preprint](https://assets-eu.researchsquare.com/files/rs-11272412/v1_covered_c7489fda-b312-42a7-8742-a1f862f8a778.pdf?c=1792740449)); this is a preprint, not yet peer reviewed. A separate study finds that almost every published LLM trading agent "fails to beat a random baseline once its knowledge cutoff is passed" ([Profit Mirage](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.07920)). The notes found no verifiable report of durable live profit from an LLM-generated strategy, and Man Group has published no hit rate or live performance.

Six failure modes recur, and the mitigations that work are structural. The first is homogeneity: one practitioner asked for 20 strategies and received 14 copies of the same mean-reversion logic with different lookbacks ([Whetlan](https://dev.to/whetlan/i-asked-an-llm-to-generate-20-trading-strategies-14-were-the-same-thing-2f36)); the defences are syntax-tree or PnL-correlation novelty gates and clustering before counting. The second is adaptive overfitting: "Whatever data its backtests run on is training data, even though no gradient was computed", so the holdout must never reach the loop, "not as a score, not as a pass/fail flag" ([waylandz](https://waylandz.com/blog/validating-llm-generated-factors/)). The third is look-ahead in generated code that runs cleanly; the defence is construction, meaning tools or a formula language whose feature space excludes the future, plus a mechanical test that alters data after a date and checks that earlier outputs do not move. The fourth is drift between idea and implementation, which Man Group names directly. The fifth is gaming the harness: one agent "moved the backtest start date on its own to get more history" ([Allan Chan](https://allanchan339.github.io/research/2026/04/24/MVP-LLM-alpha-mining.html)), and another raised Sharpe by cutting risk, scoring 0.896 in sample and 0.23 on a sealed test ([ialberquilla](https://ialberquilla.substack.com/p/how-to-make-an-ai-agent-rewrite-its)). The sixth is contamination: the model's sense of what works comes from published anomalies that have since weakened, and it has memorised market history, so certified evaluation belongs after the training cutoff.

The practitioner reports that end well share a shape. In one, Claude Code searched USD/JPY with the last 20% of data hidden server-side, a ledger of every trial and a deflated Sharpe in every response. After 13 trials the agent read its result as "within chance", stopped searching, ran sensitivity checks, wrote down a prediction, and then saw a holdout return of +1.2%; it concluded "I would not call this a working strategy" ([hawkbtcommander](https://dev.to/hawkbtcommander/i-let-claude-code-search-for-a-trading-strategy-but-hid-the-last-two-years-of-data-from-it-48ee)). A former fund operator had Claude build a production-grade pipeline in days, and "the signal came out flat" ([Peter Wild](https://petertwild.com/writing/llm-alpha-how-not-what)). These are blog accounts and probably biased toward authors willing to publish negative results, but they agree with the audits: the agent is a strong engineer and a disciplined falsifier when the harness tells it the truth, and a confident fabricator when it does not. One author's Claude-built backtests "told me they were" profitable; of 105 filter groups tested later, "Zero survived" ([TradingEdge Journal](https://tradingedgejournal.com/blog/building-trading-tools-with-claude)).

## Implications for the factory's design

These implications are the synthesis of the research, not sourced findings, and they stop short of an architecture.

The gate comes first and is indifferent to where an idea came from. Every institutional example puts the controls in the evaluation process, and Man Group applies its existing research gate unchanged to machine output. The evaluator should be deterministic code with thresholds fixed in advance, and it should own the windows, costs, universe and metrics so that an agent cannot alter them. An LLM critic can offer a second opinion; it should not issue verdicts.

The trial ledger is the foundation of any confidence statement. Deflated Sharpe, PBO and batch-level tests all require every trial's return series, including failures, and all understate overfitting when trials go unrecorded. The same ledger supports a per-dataset trial budget and reveals when a dataset is exhausted.

"Winner" needs redefining. The evidence favours many weak, low-correlation, explainable signals combined with near-equal weights and volatility targeting, judged by marginal contribution to the combined book after costs. A core of slow trend and carry across 15 to 30 instruments, with mined strategies as satellites, matches both the evidence and free daily data.

Search space should be shaped by priors. Pre-registered, mechanism-backed hypotheses on slow premia merit lighter hurdles than brute search, which the 2026 audit states explicitly. Short-horizon price patterns, unconstrained search and machine-learning discoveries need the strictest hurdles. Strategies whose edge per trade is a few spreads, or whose holding period is shorter than the bar, should be excluded outright as fill-model artefacts.

Leakage has to be prevented by construction because statistics cannot detect it. A restricted point-in-time data interface or formula language, a future-perturbation test on every candidate, and reproduction of a known published result before trusting the harness are the three sourced defences.

Paper trading should run on the factory's own cost model. Broker paper profit is an upper bound, so each paper fill needs re-pricing with spread, fees and impact, and the paper stage should be framed as an implementation and consistency check with a parallel portfolio-level record, not as proof of skill.

Decay handling should favour pausing over killing. In a paper-only setting a false kill costs little, so allocation can be cut quickly while the strategy keeps running in shadow and collecting evidence. Refits belong to combination weights on long windows, and regime models to diagnosis.

Success measures should reward correct rejections and calibrated predictions. The base rate of real discoveries is very low, and a factory measured by the number of strategies it passes will optimise for false positives.

## Open questions for the next phase

The first question is what "a stated level of confidence" will mean. The research supports several candidates (a deflated Sharpe above 0.95, a false-discovery rate across accepted strategies, a PBO ceiling per search campaign, a single-look holdout pass), and they answer different questions. No source gives an agreed PBO threshold, and no study measures the false-discovery rate of a complete multi-stage funnel, so the factory's stated confidence will itself need validation, for example by running the whole pipeline on synthetic data with a known edge.

The second is how to keep the holdout honest over time. A sealed holdout that also falls after the model's training cutoff is short today and grows only as the calendar advances; cumulative trials on fixed history grow without limit. Whether to ration trials, rotate in new markets, or treat the paper period as the true final holdout is undecided.

The third is the universe. ETFs, FX and spot crypto are reachable with free data and free paper venues from Australia. Single stocks need paid survivorship-free data, and crypto funding carry lacks a confirmed Australian paper venue; Bybit's status, Binance futures demo access and ASX data all need checking. The diversification available from an ETF-only universe, as opposed to the futures universes in the cited studies, is unmeasured.

The fourth is how long paper confirmation lasts and what it decides. If a strategy at Sharpe 1 needs three years for individual significance, the promotion rule after 6 to 12 months must rest on consistency with the predicted distribution and on aggregate evidence, and both the rule and its error rates need designing. No primary study guides incubation length or sequential monitoring.

The fifth is the form strategies take. A constrained formula language makes novelty checks, complexity caps and leak prevention cheap, at the cost of expressiveness; free-form Python is flexible and needs separate machinery for each control. A related choice is how much feedback the agent sees, since rich feedback speeds learning and also turns validation data into training data.

The sixth is the human role. No source quantifies how much human review a solo LLM factory needs to stay robust. Man Group keeps an investment committee in the loop; a solo operator has to decide which promotions get a human look and how to avoid becoming the channel through which holdout knowledge leaks back into the search.

## Conclusion

The research reframes the goal. A factory that "finds winners" in the sense of surfacing high-Sharpe backtests is easy to build and worthless; the evidence says its output would be indistinguishable from chance. A factory that can say "this process, run this many times on this data, admits false strategies at no more than this rate, and here is the ledger" is harder, buildable with free tools, and rare even among published systems. Its fast loop is not only backtest and optimise but backtest, count and deflate.

The second shift concerns time. The slowest part of the process cannot be accelerated: evidence about a live edge accrues at the square root of time. The design responses that remain are breadth (many low-correlation strategies whose aggregate record matures sooner), honesty about what a few months of paper trading shows, and patience built into the promotion rules. For an operator whose agents will mostly rediscover published, fast-decaying ideas, the durable asset is the evaluation process itself.
