# How quant firms run the strategy lifecycle, and what a solo automated factory can copy

Scope: research organisation, approval, deployment/incubation, capital allocation, monitoring, regime adaptation, decay detection and retirement. Written as input for designing a solo-developer, paper-trading-only "strategy mining factory" (Nov 2026). Source-quality labels: **[primary]** = firm/author's own publication or peer-reviewed paper; **[secondary]** = journalist/book summary or third-party write-up; **[anecdotal/blog]** = practitioner blog, unverified.

---

## Q1. How do firms organise alpha research (pipelines, committees, idea logging, alpha factories, reproducibility)?

### Takeaway
There are two dominant models: (a) **centralised "one model / research factory"** shops (Renaissance, Two Sigma, D.E. Shaw, Man AHL, Winton) where many specialists feed signals into one shared, centrally combined system with peer review and an investment committee; and (b) **pod / alpha-marketplace** shops (Millennium, Citadel, WorldQuant BRAIN) where independent units or crowdsourced "consultants" submit alphas that must pass hard, mechanical gates before a central risk layer allocates capital. Both converge on the same core: hypothesis → statistical test → cost-aware simulation → held-out test → gatekeeping by someone other than the researcher → central combination.

### Cited Findings

**Centralised / single-model shops**
- Renaissance's Medallion used a **single monolithic trading system**; all staff could read all source code and "anyone could make experimental modifications to improve the trading system". Combining all signals and portfolio requirements in one model meant they "could easily test and add new signals, instantly knowing if the gains from a potential new strategy were likely to top its costs." [secondary: Zuckerman book quotes] — [Novel Investor notes on Zuckerman](https://novelinvestor.com/notes/the-man-who-solved-the-market-by-gregory-zuckerman); [A Wealth of Common Sense](https://awealthofcommonsense.com/2019/11/non-intuitive-lessons-from-the-man-who-solved-the-market)
- RenTec's three-step signal process: identify anomalous patterns in historical data; check they are statistically significant, consistent over time and non-random; see if they can be explained reasonably. Signals with p-values under 0.01 were added even if not obviously explicable; by 1997 more than half of new signals were non-intuitive, but obviously nonsensical ones (e.g. "tickers starting with A") were excluded. [secondary] — [Novel Investor](https://novelinvestor.com/notes/the-man-who-solved-the-market-by-gregory-zuckerman); [Twoquants summary](https://twoquants.com/the-man-who-solved-the-market-part-1)
- RenTec's edge was many small bets: Mercer: "We're right 50.75 percent of the time ... but we're 100 percent right 50.75 percent of the time." Zuckerman notes this was likely illustrative, not the exact edge. [secondary] — [Zuckerman book text](https://mathquantphi.com/Others/Books/Bk_The_Man_Who_Solved_the_Market.pdf)
- RenTec's system was adaptive: "A consistent winner ... might automatically receive more cash, without anyone approving the shift"; in 2000 a once-trusted momentum signal bled money and researchers had to diagnose it manually. Simons overrode the model in rare crises (e.g. 2007 quant crash) — "99% of the time" they let the model run. [secondary] — [Twoquants part 2](https://twoquants.com/the-man-who-solved-the-market-part-2); [Rational Reminder interview with Zuckerman](https://rationalreminder.ca/podcast/97)
- Pay at RenTec is tied to Medallion as a whole, encouraging collaboration ("I don't mind getting a cup of coffee for somebody if it means helping Medallion improve"). [secondary] — [Business of Business interview](https://www.businessofbusiness.com/articles/jim-simons-renaissance-technologies-rentech-gregory-zuckerman-quant)
- Two Sigma describes a 4-stage process: data sourcing & preparation → modelling → portfolio construction → execution, with a "rigorous modeling process to minimize overfitting". [primary] — [Two Sigma Investment Management](https://www.twosigma.com/businesses/investment-management). Two Sigma also runs randomised A/B trials of algorithm versions in production over "days, weeks, and months". [primary] — [Two Sigma: Curiosity x Scientific Method](https://www.twosigma.com/articles/the-innovation-equation-curiosity-x-the-scientific-method-at-two-sigma)
- Citadel (Equities Quant Research) on alpha research: "ideas become cheap ... What really matters is executing and prioritizing among them." Process: articulate the data-generating mechanism and how to disprove it → measure strength/consistency → simulate with realistic costs → final test on held-out data used "only near the end of the process". [primary] — [Citadel: Inside EQR](https://www.citadel.com/careers/career-perspectives/inside-eqr-alpha-research)
- Winton/AHL (David Harding): research improvements need "a peer group review of some rigour", must then be validated by someone else, and a further resource-allocation decision is made about whether a small improvement is worth integrating into the whole system. [secondary: interview] — [Hedge Fund Journal on Winton](https://thehedgefundjournal.com/winton-capital-management). Winton in 2020 aligned senior researchers with specific strategies, making them "accountable for each strategy's relevant research, that strategy's integration into the investment system and its ongoing management." [primary] — [Winton research update](https://www.belmontinvestments.com/cimg/file/articles/58/pdf/200101wintonresearchupdate.pdf)
- Man Group: change-management controls are applied "to new models, model changes and calibrations" (preventative and detective). [primary: 2025 annual report] — [Man Group Annual Report 2025](https://www.man.com/document?doc-type=cap&locale=en)
- **Man AHL's AlphaGPT (2025)**: agentic system with idea-generator, implementer and "Evaluator" agents; every AI-produced signal "must demonstrate clear economic rationale and pass identical evaluation thresholds" as human research; full logging "from initial hypothesis through final implementation"; dual-track validation — the **Investment Committee** reviews hypothesis/rationale/results while tech teams do code review, unit/integration/scenario tests. Man names p-hacking from rapid variation testing and "the AI might conceptualise one research idea but implement something different" as key risks. [primary] — [Man Group: What AI Can (and Can't Yet) Do for Alpha](https://www.man.com/insights/what-ai-can-do-for-alpha)
- Man on automated search: "A typical quant may discover one signal a month ... a machine can discover hundreds"; defence against data mining is to compare found alphas to what random noise would produce ("tried 10,000 formulas and found 10 that look good ... you'd expect to find this in random noise"), blend rather than cherry-pick, check OOS/IS ratio, then test the combination on unseen data. [primary] — [Man Group: AI Diary of a Quant](https://www.man.com/insights/the-big-picture-ai)
- López de Prado's **"meta-strategy paradigm"**: "It takes almost as much effort to produce one true investment strategy as to produce a hundred"; "every successful quantitative firm I am aware of applies the meta-strategy paradigm" — an assembly line with stations: Data Curators → Feature Analysts (catalogue libraries of findings, not strategies) → Strategists (a strategy is "the experiment designed to test the validity of [a] theory"; must name "the economic mechanism that causes an agent to lose money to us") → Backtesters (must evaluate probability of backtest overfitting "taking into account the number of trials it took"; results go "to management and not shared with anyone else") → Deployment team (ensures production is "logically identical to the prototype") → Portfolio Oversight. [primary: book excerpt] — [AFML ch.1 excerpt (Wiley)](https://catalogimages.wiley.com/images/db/pdf/9781119482086.excerpt.pdf); [10 Reasons Most ML Funds Fail](https://www.garp.org/hubfs/Whitepapers/a1Z1W0000054x6lUAA.pdf)

**Pod / alpha-factory shops**
- Pod shops (Millennium, Citadel, Balyasny, Point72) are federations of small semi-autonomous teams under a central risk overlay; "Individual pods blow up regularly. The platform almost never does, because no single pod is allowed to matter." [secondary/blog] — [Quant GT](https://quantgt.io/research/how-quant-firms-operate); [AlphaNova blog](https://www.alphanova.tech/blog/what-is-a-quant-pod) (pod size "3–8 people", PM payout "15–20%" — secondary).
- WorldQuant is described as a hybrid: researchers submit alphas to a central library (collaborative research), PMs select and combine them (pod-like risk taking); capacity of strong alphas is competed for internally. [anecdotal/blog] — [AlphaNova](https://www.alphanova.tech/blog/what-is-a-quant-pod)
- **WorldQuant BRAIN** submission gates (public user thresholds, Delay-1): Sharpe > 1.25, Fitness > 1.0 where Fitness = Sharpe × sqrt(|Returns| / max(Turnover, 0.125)), 1% < turnover < 70%, max weight per stock < 10%, sub-universe test (must still work in the more liquid sub-universe), **self-correlation < 0.7** to your existing alphas (or ≥10% higher Sharpe than the correlated one). Only submitted alphas then accrue out-of-sample tracking. [primary docs, mirrored] — [BRAIN docs mirror](http://61.182.4.91:8765/wiki/view?path=raw%2Farticles%2Fworldquant-brain-learn-docs%2Fraw%2Fclear-these-tests-before-submitting-an-alpha.md); [Fitness formula](http://61.182.4.91:8765/wiki/view?path=raw%2Farticles%2Fworldquant-brain-learn-docs%2Fraw%2Fparameters-in-the-simulation-results.md)
- Consultant tier is stricter: Sharpe > 1.58 (D1), plus **prod-correlation** (< 0.7 vs all alphas on BRAIN, not just your own), an **IS-ladder test** (Sharpe over the most recent 2,3,...10 years must each clear threshold), and a forward-bias test. "Power Pool" alphas: ≤ 8 operators, ≤ 3 data fields — explicit complexity caps. [primary docs, mirrored on GitHub] — [consultant-submission-tests.md](https://github.com/Lovevaishnav0820/brain-mcp-docs/blob/main/consultant-submission-tests.md)
- "101 Formulaic Alphas" (WorldQuant): real production alphas with holding periods 0.6–6.4 days, median Sharpe ~2.2, **average pairwise correlation 15.9%** — i.e. the factory produces many weak, low-correlated signals. [primary] — [Kakushadze et al., Wilmott 2016](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/wilm.10525)
- WorldQuant's *Finding Alphas*: compare IS vs OOS "not only on the alpha level but also in aggregate across all alphas from a given designer ... [to] measure the tendency of a designer's methodology to overfit"; after an automated search, judge **the whole batch** — e.g. 100 alphas with average OOS IR 0.01 should all be rejected "including the 60 with positive out-of-sample performance." [primary: book] — [Finding Alphas excerpt](https://jchenmir.github.io/download/Finding_Alphas__A_Quantitative_Approach_to_Building_Trading_Strategies-Igor_Tulchinsky_et_al.pdf)

**Idea logging / record keeping**
- Carver: "Record all backtests that are run"; ideally keep a "fund level" backtest in which "every single idea we've ever considered is stored" and gets a risk allocation, so dropping failed ideas doesn't inflate history. Dropping unprofitable EWMAC variants inflated a backtest SR from 0.17 to 0.27 with nearly identical final models. [primary: author blog/talk] — [Carver: three kinds of overfitting](https://qoppac.blogspot.com/2015/11/the-three-kinds-of-overfitting.html?m=0); [Carver Thalesians slides](https://cmtassociation.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/01/Robert-Carver-1.pdf)
- A practitioner white paper proposes explicit state discipline: idea → testing → rejected/frozen/watchlist → approved for implementation review → shadow → live → blocked/killed; material changes create new versions requiring renewed review; "Rejections, blockers and killed signals ... are part of the evidence base." [secondary/practitioner, small firm] — [Guerrieri Capital: From Signal Research to Capital Use](https://guerriericapital.com/papers/from-signal-research-to-capital-use.pdf)

### Inferences
- The universal pattern is **separation of roles**: the generator of an idea is never the one who approves it, and backtest results are evaluated with knowledge of how many trials produced them. In a solo factory, LLM agents can play generator/implementer, but the evaluator/gatekeeper should be deterministic code with pre-registered thresholds, not another LLM's opinion.
- WorldQuant BRAIN is the closest public template for a mechanical "alpha factory": expression-language alphas, fixed gate metrics, correlation-to-library gates, complexity caps, and OOS tracking only after submission. It is directly copyable.
- RenTec's "single model" idea maps to: every new signal is evaluated by its **marginal contribution to the combined portfolio after costs**, not standalone.

### Gaps
- No primary documentation of internal research-committee procedures at RenTec, Two Sigma, AQR or Citadel beyond public career pages; Millennium/WorldQuant internal relationship details are blog-level.
- Narang's *Inside the Black Box* not reached within the fetch budget.

## Q2. Production: backtest → incubation/paper → small capital → full capital; allocation across many alphas; combining weak signals

### Takeaway
The canonical path (López de Prado) is **embargo → paper trading → graduation (small real allocation) → automatic re-allocation on a concave curve → decommission**. Capital across alphas is allocated centrally, favouring diversification and low correlation; robust weighting (averaging, shrinkage, bootstrapping) beats picking the "best" backtest.

### Cited Findings
- López de Prado's *cursus honorum*: (1) **Embargo** — run on data after the backtest end date; promoted if consistent; (2) **Paper trading** — live real-time feed, capturing parsing/calculation/execution latencies, "for as long as it is needed to gather enough evidence"; (3) **Graduation** — manages a real position, alone or in an ensemble, with attributed risk, returns and costs; (4) **Re-allocation** — "re-assessed frequently and automatically in the context of a diversified portfolio ... allocation follows a concave function. The initial allocation ... is small. As time passes, and the strategy performs as expected, the allocation is increased. Over time, performance decays, and allocations become gradually smaller"; (5) **Decommission** — "all strategies are discontinued" when they underperform "for a sufficiently extended period of time to conclude that the supporting theory is no longer backed by empirical evidence". New versions run in parallel with old ones, each going through the lifecycle. [primary] — [AFML ch.1 excerpt](https://catalogimages.wiley.com/images/db/pdf/9781119482086.excerpt.pdf)
- Man AlphaGPT: "Whether generated by human or AI, every strategy entering live trading must pass identical thresholds." [primary] — [Man Group](https://www.man.com/insights/what-ai-can-do-for-alpha)
- Practitioner review outcomes: approve for live under limits / approve for **shadow monitoring only** / conditional approval / return to watchlist / block / reject; minimum evidence file includes thesis, data lineage, point-in-time validation, rejected variants, robustness, cost & capacity, sizing, **monitoring thresholds and revision rules** written before deployment. [secondary] — [Guerrieri Capital](https://guerriericapital.com/papers/from-signal-research-to-capital-use.pdf)
- RenTec: a single optimiser runs "on a loop several times an hour", weighing thousands of potential trades given costs, leverage and risk; "Every bet is a function of all the other bets." [secondary] — [Twoquants part 2](https://twoquants.com/the-man-who-solved-the-market-part-2); [Zuckerman text](https://mathquantphi.com/Others/Books/Bk_The_Man_Who_Solved_the_Market.pdf). Adding signals from new markets lowered correlation to markets and smoothed returns. [secondary] — same source.
- Carver: "Trading rule returns are pretty noisy ... it's really hard to pick out which one is the best. And by far the best approach is just to take an average" — of reasonably distinct (corr < ~0.95) variations. [secondary: podcast summary] — [Better System Trader #26](https://bettersystemtrader.com/026-robert-carver). He fits forecast weights by bootstrapping on an expanding window and pools data across instruments. [primary] — [Carver: simulating my futures system](https://qoppac.blogspot.com/2015/03/simulating-my-futures-system.html). Robust fitting "is usually a tilt rather than a wholesale reallocation." [primary] — [Carver: three kinds of overfitting](https://qoppac.blogspot.com/2015/11/the-three-kinds-of-overfitting.html)
- Carver's own system: ~45 instruments, 8 rules / 4 styles, 30 variations, backtest SR ~1.0 after costs; recommends half-Kelly using a pessimistic SR; "none [of a group of systematic funds] could sustain a Sharpe ratio above 1.0 for more than a few years." [secondary: book notes] — [Carver notes](https://tfnsnews.blogspot.com/2025/05/systematic-trading-carver-notes.html); [CXO Advisory](https://www.cxoadvisory.com/big-ideas/a-few-notes-on-systematic-trading/)
- AQR (Asness, 2024): a genuinely new signal "will be at least somewhat diversifying ... meaning you can add more of it than what you take out of the existing signals"; evolve the process as "good Bayesians ... combination of our priors and live results." [primary] — [AQR CIO Perspectives interview](https://www.aqr.com/-/media/AQR/Documents/Journal-Articles/AQR-JPM-CIO-Perspective-Interview-with-Cliff-Asness.pdf?sc_lang=en)
- Combining signals carries its own overfitting: combining the best k of n random signals yields bias almost as large as picking the single best of n^k; two signals with t=1.5 or five with t=1 combine to "significant" backtests. [primary: NBER] — [Novy-Marx, Backtesting Strategies Based on Multiple Signals](https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w21329/w21329.pdf)
- Kakushadze & Yu give an explicit algorithm for combining very large numbers of alphas (dimension-reduction because sample covariance of many alphas is near-singular). [primary] — [How to Combine a Billion Alphas](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1603.05937); singularity note in [101 Alphas summary](https://github.com/jglazar/notes/blob/main/quant_interview/alpha_ideas.md)
- Pod-shop central allocation monitors rolling Sharpe, drawdown from allocated capital, and factor overlap across pods. [secondary/blog] — [Young and Calculated](https://youngandcalculated.substack.com/p/how-multi-manager-hedge-funds-actually)

### Inferences
- For paper-only trading, the lifecycle collapses to: **embargo/holdout → paper incubation (shadow) → "virtual graduation"** where the strategy gets a notional allocation in a combined paper portfolio, with allocation following a concave ramp and decay. This is fully automatable.
- Allocation should be ensemble/averaging-based with correlation penalties, not "promote the top backtest".

### Gaps
- No public numbers on how long firms keep strategies in paper/incubation (weeks vs months) or what allocation ramp they use; López de Prado only says "as long as it is needed".

## Q3. Monitoring and adaptation: decay measurement, live-vs-backtest tracking, kill rules, regime detection, refit cadence, refit vs retire, ensemble re-weighting

### Takeaway
Firms prefer **slow, statistically disciplined adaptation**: expanding/rolling-window refits of combination weights, gradual allocation reduction as evidence of decay builds, and hard pre-committed drawdown limits at the risk-overlay level. Regime-switching and factor timing are treated sceptically by AQR and Carver; regime models are mainly used as **risk/description tools**, not alpha.

### Cited Findings

**Refitting (Carver)**
- "No time machine": only test parameters on data after the fit period; choose expanding window (slow, low-SR effects like trend following that "will also hopefully not go away") or rolling window (HFT-like, "a few months", high-SR effects "quickly degraded once the competition finds out"). [primary] — [Carver: three kinds of overfitting](https://qoppac.blogspot.com/2015/11/the-three-kinds-of-overfitting.html?m=0); [Carver slides](https://cmtassociation.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/01/Robert-Carver-1.pdf)
- Carver: implicit fitting (tweaking after seeing OOS, changing hyperparameters until happy, discarding ideas) is cheating; "Don't try and 'polish coal'; Don't 'improve' (overfit) if performance okay." [primary] — [Carver Systematic Trading slides](https://cmtassociation.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/01/0302-carver-1.pdf)
- Statistical power: at SR 0.5 it takes ~10 years to pass a t-test that SR > 0; "the average trading rule for one instrument has a realistic SR of around 0.3; so you'd need nearly 40 years"; distinguishing SR 0.3 vs 0.8 takes ~3 decades. [secondary: book summaries] — [7 Circles on Carver](https://the7circles.uk/systematic-trading-2-fitting-and-allocation); [Carver notes](https://tfnsnews.blogspot.com/2025/05/systematic-trading-carver-notes.html). Carver: "A year is a very, very short period of time to actually find any sort of statistical evidence"; "at least ten years" to judge profitability. [secondary] — [Better System Trader](https://bettersystemtrader.com/026-robert-carver)
- Carver keeps poorly performing rules in the backtest and lets the optimiser down-weight them only "in as much as there was statistically significant evidence"; he adds rules only if they give "a statistically significant improvement" (exceptions for simplifying/diversifying changes). [primary] — [Carver: Some more trading rules](https://qoppac.blogspot.com/2017/06/some-more-trading-rules.html)
- Carver uses long half-lives for performance estimation (e.g. 15-year half-life on alphas, betas re-estimated yearly) and found "none of the fancy alternatives ... add value"; tried and rejected "setting risk using drawdown levels (too simple!)". [primary] — [Carver March 2024](https://qoppac.blogspot.com/2024/03?m=1); [Carver: My trading system](https://qoppac.blogspot.com/2021/12/my-trading-system.html?m=0)

**Kill rules / drawdown rules**
- Millennium (reported): a PM given $1bn who loses $50m (5%) likely has buying power halved; a further $25m (7.5% total) likely means firing; PM turnover ~15–20%/yr. [secondary: WSJ] — [WSJ](https://www.wsj.com/finance/investing/the-giant-hedge-fund-that-hates-risk-and-still-wins-1110e90a); [Markets Insider](https://markets.businessinsider.com/news/stocks/hedge-fund-strategy-never-lose-money-in-the-stock-market-2024-10). Thresholds are single-sourced (Net Interest, 2023) and the reference point (high-water mark vs window) is undisclosed; Citadel/Point72 negotiate per PM. [secondary] — [Hedge Fund Interview](https://hedgefundinterview.com/pod-shop-risk-limits)
- CFM (Capital Fund Management) research: drawdowns are routinely underestimated — "there is a 5% chance for the drawdown of a ten-year process with SR = 0.5 to last 7 years or more"; 5% drawdown length ≈ 2.14 × SR⁻² years. [primary] — [CFM: You are in a drawdown. When should you start worrying?](https://www.cfm.com/wp-content/uploads/2022/12/317-2017-You-are-in-a-drawdown-When-should-you-start-worrying.pdf)
- Drawdown limits should be adjusted for time managed (longer track → more chance of bad luck) and for relative cost of Type I (firing good) vs Type II (keeping bad) errors. [primary, abstract only] — [Drawdowns, JPM 2020](https://www.pm-research.com/content/iijpormgmt/46/8/34)
- A simulation (blog) of common kill rules (halve on rolling-90d Sharpe < backtest 5th pct or DD > 1× backtest max; pause on DD > 1.5× max): a healthy SR 1.0 strategy gets halved 81% and paused 33% of the time within 3 years, while a zero-edge strategy runs unpaused through year one 82% of the time — "The rules react to everything a little and to nothing in particular." A single backtest's max DD is exceeded by another same-length path ~51% of the time. [anecdotal/blog, but useful] — [Backtesting Arena](https://tradingstrategies.work/blog/trading-kill-rules-false-alarms)
- Better practice described by practitioners: benchmark live vs **out-of-sample** (not in-sample) expectancy using standard errors; graded response (≈2 SE below → halve size; 2.5–3 SE or DD beyond Monte Carlo 95th percentile → stop); required trades n ≈ (2·SD/E)². [anecdotal/blog] — [Alpha Strategic Growth](https://www.alphastrategicgrowth.com/blog/alpha-decay-trading); Monte Carlo prediction envelope for live equity curve — [Kinlay](https://jonathankinlay.com/2018/11/strategy-still-working); kill switches written before deployment (P95 DD, longest underwater, rolling R) — [Inside Systematic](https://insidesystematic.substack.com/p/what-happens-after-a-strategy-goes)
- RenTec reinforced after LTCM: "Never place too much trust in trading models ... If a strategy wasn't working, or when market volatility surged, Renaissance's system tended to automatically reduce positions and risk." [secondary] — [Novel Investor](https://novelinvestor.com/notes/the-man-who-solved-the-market-by-gregory-zuckerman)

**Regime detection / timing**
- AQR: "Factor timing is difficult, and a consistent and disciplined exposure to a well-diversified multifactor portfolio is hard to beat"; internal research trying to add factor timing found it "extremely challenging". [primary] — [AQR: Fact, Fiction, and Factor Investing (2023)](https://www.aqr.com/-/media/AQR/Documents/Journal-Articles/AQRJPMQuant23FactFictionandFactorInvesting.pdf?sc_lang=en). Asness recommends "sinning a little"; chasing 3–5-year recent performance "is a recipe for very poor results." [primary] — [AQR: Factor Timing is Hard](https://www.aqr.com/Insights/Perspectives/Factor-Timing-is-Hard). Tactical tilts carry a "forgone diversification" penalty, so required hit-rate rises with tilt size and asset diversification. [primary] — [AQR: Tactical Tilts and Forgone Diversification](https://www.aqr.com/-/media/AQR/Documents/Insights/Alternative-Thinking/Tactical-Tilts-and-Foregone-Diversification.pdf). But breadth helps: deep-value timing across many cross-sections did show alpha. [primary] — [AQR: Going Deep on Contrarian Factor Timing](https://www.aqr.com/Insights/Perspectives/Going-Deep-on-Contrarian-Factor-Timing)
- Two Sigma published a Gaussian Mixture Model on its 17-factor lens yielding four conditions (Crisis, Steady State, Inflation, "Walking on Ice"), framed as a tool to understand how portfolios react to regimes, not a trading signal. [primary] — [Two Sigma: ML Approach to Regime Modeling](https://www.twosigma.com/articles/a-machine-learning-approach-to-regime-modeling)
- Academic evidence on HMM/regime allocation is mixed: some OOS gains mainly as downside-risk reduction (statistical jump model beats HMM by enforcing persistence) — [Shu, Yu, Mulvey 2024](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1057/s41260-024-00376-x); a rolling 24-month HMM switched regime in 55 of 56 OOS months and regime-agnostic allocation did better — [arXiv 2610.27751](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.27751v2); HMM regime allocation didn't beat 1/N; "best interpreted as risk-management tools rather than universal return-enhancing solutions" — [ScienceDirect 2026](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S105905602600479X); raw HMMs produce short, noisy regimes that raise trading costs unless smoothed — [Blanchard 2025](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/asmb.70058).
- Crowding signals predict **tail/crash risk, not average returns**: crowded reversal factors had 1.7–1.8× crash probability; crowding-timed selection Sharpe 0.22 vs 0.39 for simple factor momentum (single-author arXiv preprint). [primary, preprint] — [Lee, Not All Factors Crowd Equally](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.11913v1)

### Inferences
- Copyable adaptation policy: (1) refit **combination weights**, not signal internals, on an expanding/long-half-life window at a slow cadence (e.g. quarterly/annually); (2) change allocation gradually per López de Prado's concave curve; (3) use volatility targeting/risk reduction as the main "regime" response; (4) treat regime classifiers as diagnostic labels for attribution and stress analysis, not as switches.
- Kill rules must be calibrated to the strategy's own OOS distribution (Monte Carlo/bootstrap) and to the number of strategies monitored; naive "1.5× backtest max DD" rules have poor discrimination. For a paper-only factory, false kills are cheap — so rules can lean aggressive on *pausing allocation* while keeping the strategy running in shadow to keep collecting evidence.

### Gaps
- No primary disclosure of refit cadence at any named firm beyond Carver.
- No firm-published live-vs-backtest tracking-error thresholds.

## Q4. Evidence on how quickly edges decay; how firms keep a pipeline of new signals

### Takeaway
Published equity anomalies lose roughly **a quarter of returns out-of-sample and about half post-publication**; commercial "alternative beta" backtests haircut ~70%. Faster/price-based and higher-in-sample-Sharpe signals decay more. Firms survive by continuously producing many low-correlation signals and pricing in overfitting (t > 3, deflated Sharpe, batch-level OOS tests).

### Cited Findings
- McLean & Pontiff (2016), 97 predictors: returns **26% lower out-of-sample, 58% lower post-publication** (≈32% attributed to publication-informed trading). Decay is larger for higher in-sample returns/t-stats and for predictors built only from price/volume data; smaller where arbitrage is costly (illiquid, high idiosyncratic risk). Post-publication, predictors' correlation with other published predictors rises. Average monthly return: 0.582% in-sample → 0.402% OOS pre-publication → 0.264% post-publication. [primary] — [J. Finance](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1111/jofi.12365); [CFA digest](https://rpc.cfainstitute.org/research/cfa-digest/2016/06/does-academic-research-destroy-stock-return-predictability-digest-summary)
- Falck, Rej & Thesmar (CFM) on 60 anomalies: post-publication Sharpe drops ~43–50%; publication year explains 30% of variance in decay and decay grows ~5pp per year of publication; overfitting proxies (number of operations to compute the signal, sensitivity to outliers) add ~15% explanatory power; "the balance of evidence is in favor of the overfitting story." [primary] — [When do systematic strategies decay? (Quant Finance 2022)](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/14697688.2022.2098810)
- Contrary view: international evidence suggests post-publication drops in the US are mainly arbitrage rather than data-mining. [primary] — [Jacobs & Müller, JFE 2020](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0304405X19301618)
- 215 bank-marketed alternative-beta strategies: median Sharpe **1.20 in backtest vs 0.31 live (73% haircut)**. [primary] — [Suhonen, Lennkh & Perez, Quantifying Backtest Overfitting in Alternative Beta Strategies](https://community.portfolio123.com/uploads/short-url/eDD8GQ0ZmwCF8vCR4dORON040Sf.pdf)
- Professional managers' new-buy alpha decays with **half-life ~4 months**, zero after a year. [primary] — [Di Mascio, Lines & Naik, Alpha Decay](https://www.top1000funds.com/wp-content/uploads/2021/05/SSRN-id2580551.pdf)
- Maven Securities: cost of execution-delay alpha decay averaged 5.6% (US) / 9.9% (Europe) and rising (~36bps/yr US). [primary, practitioner] — [Maven](https://www.mavensecurities.com/alpha-decay-what-does-it-look-like-and-what-does-it-mean-for-systematic-traders)
- Momentum factor return ~10%/yr in 1990s vs ~2% now; hyperbolic decay fits mechanical factors; crowding accelerated post-2015. [primary, preprint] — [Lee 2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.11913v1). A 2026 arXiv paper claims factor half-lives shrank from ~60 to ~18 months — but this is a **calibrated simulation**, treat as weak. — [arXiv 2605.23905](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.23905)
- Multiple-testing hurdles: Harvey, Liu & Zhu advocate t > 3.0; a 2026 NBER paper again finds t ≥ 3.0 lower bound and recommends local FDR. [primary] — [Harvey, Sancetta & Zhao, NBER w34898](https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w34898/w34898.pdf). Counterpoint: Chen argues most published findings are likely true (FDR ≤ 25%), the dispute is interpretive. [primary, preprint] — [Chen](https://arxiv.org/html/2206.15365v10). Haircut Sharpe framework cites AHL internal research on probability of backtest overfitting (PBO 0.45 in an example). [primary] — [Harvey & Liu, Evaluating Trading Strategies](https://people.duke.edu/~charvey/Research/Published_Papers/P116_Evaluating_trading_strategies.pdf)
- Pipeline mechanisms: RenTec's database grows "more than 40 terabytes a day" (secondary) and research expanded to new markets/countries to add diversifying signals [secondary] — [Algo Advantage](https://www.algoadvantage.io/podcast/019-medallion-fund); [Zuckerman text](https://mathquantphi.com/Others/Books/Bk_The_Man_Who_Solved_the_Market.pdf). RenTec trades strongest signals "to capacity" partly to hide them from competitors (secondary, same source). WorldQuant crowdsources via BRAIN (primary — [WorldQuant BRAIN](https://www.worldquant.com/brain/)). Medallion capped at ~$10bn to protect capacity (secondary — [Rational Reminder](https://rationalreminder.ca/podcast/97)).

### Inferences
- Design assumption for the factory: expect any accepted signal's live Sharpe to be **≈ 50% of its honest OOS Sharpe** (and ~25–30% of a naive backtest's), with faster-decay for simple price/volume rules. Budget for continuous replacement; the "half-life" of a price-based retail-accessible signal should be assumed to be months-to-few-years, not decades.
- Because LLM agents will mostly rediscover published/price-volume ideas (the fastest-decaying class), the factory's real edge is process (honest evaluation, diversification, cost realism), not novelty.

### Gaps
- No reliable, firm-published statistic on internal signal half-lives or retirement rates.

## Q5. Which practices are realistically automatable by a solo developer with LLM agents?

### Takeaway
Nearly all of the *mechanical* lifecycle is automatable (generation, implementation, gated evaluation, correlation dedup, paper incubation, allocation, monitoring, kill rules, logging). What's hard to automate is the role of independent judgment (economic rationale review, catching implementation-vs-idea drift, tacit overfitting) and, fundamentally, **statistical power**: a paper-trading solo setup cannot gather enough live data to verify low-Sharpe strategies within a reasonable time.

### Cited Findings
- LLM alpha-mining systems exist and work in benchmarks: Alpha-GPT reached top-5–10% of humans in a high-frequency competition in one day vs a month, and placed near the top-10 worldwide in WorldQuant IQC 2024 Stage 2 (81 qualified alphas). [primary] — [Alpha-GPT (EMNLP 2025 demo)](https://aclanthology.org/2025.emnlp-demos.14.pdf); [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2308.00016v2)
- Microsoft's R&D-Agent(Q) automates factor and model co-optimisation; uses health checks for leakage/sparsity/redundancy, deduplication against existing factors, and a bandit scheduler to balance factor vs model exploration. [primary] — [R&D-Agent-Quant](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.15155)
- Later systems (AlphaAgent, QuantaAlpha, FactorMiner, AlphaAgentEvo) add generation-stage regularisation, evolutionary mutation/crossover and experience memory, reporting higher IC than RD-Agent; known failure modes include "context explosion and feedback drift" and "search stagnation" (models repeat familiar operator patterns). [primary, mostly Chinese A-share benchmarks; results not independently replicated] — [QuantaAlpha](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.07085); [Reinforcement fine-tuning paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.15412v1); [FactorMiner](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.14670v2)
- Man AHL treats AI-specific risks (p-hacking via fast variation, hallucination, idea-vs-implementation drift) as engineering problems: consistency checks across stages, complete decision logs, unchanged thresholds, human Investment Committee review, plus expanded monitoring for higher signal volume. [primary] — [Man Group](https://www.man.com/insights/what-ai-can-do-for-alpha)
- López de Prado: backtesters must account for number of trials; deflated Sharpe ratio and combinatorial purged CV are his tools. [primary] — [AFML excerpt](https://catalogimages.wiley.com/images/db/pdf/9781119482086.excerpt.pdf)
- WorldQuant: judge auto-search output as a **batch** (average OOS of all generated alphas), not by cherry-picking OOS winners. [primary] — [Finding Alphas](https://jchenmir.github.io/download/Finding_Alphas__A_Quantitative_Approach_to_Building_Trading_Strategies-Igor_Tulchinsky_et_al.pdf)

### Inferences (proposed copy map for the factory)
| Institutional practice | Automatable solo? | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Hypothesis memo with mechanism/counterparty/falsification (Citadel, López de Prado, Harding) | Yes (LLM writes it), but judging quality is weak | Require structured memo before any backtest; store it |
| Assembly-line stations (data → feature → strategy → backtest → deploy → oversight) | Yes, as separate agents/modules | Evaluator must be deterministic code, not the generating LLM |
| Trial registry / "record every backtest" (Carver, LdP) | Yes, trivially | Needed for deflated Sharpe and batch-level stats |
| BRAIN-style gates (Sharpe, fitness, turnover, concentration, sub-universe, IS-ladder, correlation < 0.7 to library, complexity caps) | Yes | Directly copyable thresholds as starting point |
| Multiple-testing hurdles (t > 3, DSR, PBO) | Yes | Compute from trial registry |
| Sealed holdout / embargo | Yes | Access log; never used during search |
| Paper incubation with live feed | Yes (it's the whole product) | Gives latency/cost realism, but little statistical power |
| Concave allocation ramp, ensemble re-weighting | Yes | Expanding-window, shrinkage/averaging, slow cadence |
| Pre-registered kill/pause rules calibrated by bootstrap | Yes | Prefer "pause allocation, keep shadowing" |
| Regime labels (GMM/HMM/vol buckets) | Yes, as diagnostics | Don't use as switches without strong OOS evidence |
| Peer review / Investment Committee | Partially | Human weekly review of promotions; LLM critic as second opinion only |
| Detecting tacit overfitting / idea-implementation drift | Hard | Code-vs-spec consistency checks help (Man, RD-Agent) |
| Proprietary data, capacity, execution alpha, scale | No | Solo factory works on public data; expect fast decay |

- Statistical power is the binding constraint: if real SR ~0.5, live paper data cannot confirm an edge for years (Carver/CFM numbers). The factory must therefore rely on **pre-deployment rigor and portfolio-level evaluation** (many strategies' aggregate live vs OOS ratio, à la Finding Alphas' designer-level comparison) rather than per-strategy live proof.
- On the user's sequencing question (research before or within double diamond): this lifecycle research naturally belongs in the first "Discover" diamond, since it defines the factory's stages and constraints; it is general enough not to presuppose a solution.

### Gaps
- No independent, out-of-sample, live-trading evidence for any LLM alpha-mining system; reported results are backtests on mostly Chinese equity benchmarks or competition scores.
- No source quantifies how much human review a solo LLM factory needs to stay robust.
