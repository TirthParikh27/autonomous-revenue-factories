# Survey of Systematic Trading Strategy Families (input to a solo-operator "strategy mining factory")

Context: solo developer, Python, paper trading only, operator in Australia, free/cheap data, daily or hourly bars, November 2026. Research was done with about 11 search calls (search excerpts only, no full-page fetches). Where a number comes from a secondary summary rather than the original paper, it is marked **[secondary]**. Where I could not find a source, the point is in Gaps or labelled as inference.

---

## Key Question 1: Which families have the strongest evidence of persistent net-of-cost returns? (with per-family profiles)

### Takeaway
The strongest and longest out-of-sample evidence is for **time-series momentum/trend following** (137 years, 67 markets, positive in 8 of the 10 worst 60/40 drawdowns) and **carry** (Sharpe about 0.8 per asset class, 1.2 diversified). **Value and cross-sectional momentum** are real but have long, deep drawdowns (value -55% from 2007 to 2020; momentum -73% to -91% crashes). Short-horizon anomalies (simple pairs trading, short-term reversal, PEAD, index inclusion, turn-of-month, classic technical rules) have **decayed to near zero after costs** in liquid US markets since about 2001 to 2006. Published anomalies lose about half their Sharpe after publication.

### Cited Findings

#### General decay and data-mining baseline (applies to every family)
- Across 97 published cross-sectional predictors, returns are 26% lower out of sample and 58% lower after publication. That implies about 32% decay from publication-informed trading. Decay is larger for predictors with higher in-sample returns, and the remaining returns concentrate in illiquid, high-idiosyncratic-risk stocks — [McLean & Pontiff 2016, JF](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1111/jofi.12365)
- Across 60 replicated anomalies, the Sharpe ratio drops by about 43% to 50% after publication. This is consistent with an overfitting model (Rej et al. 2019) — [Falck, Rej & Thesmar 2022, Quantitative Finance "When do systematic strategies decay?"](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/14697688.2022.2098810)
- AQR's reading: much of the performance survives out of sample, so many factors are "real", but smaller than claimed — [AQR "Fact, Fiction, and Factor Investing" 2023](https://www.aqr.com/-/media/AQR/Documents/Journal-Articles/AQRJPMQuant23FactFictionandFactorInvesting.pdf?sc_lang=en)
- Harvey, Liu & Zhu propose a double-bootstrap t-statistic hurdle tied to a chosen false discovery rate, because multiple testing plagues factor and fund selection — [Harvey & Liu 2020, JF "False (and Missed) Discoveries"](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/jofi.12951). (The widely quoted "t > 3.0" hurdle comes from the earlier Harvey-Liu-Zhu 2016 RFS paper. I did not retrieve that paper here **[unverified in this session]**.)
- Statistical power is a problem even for trend following: one study estimates that about 250 years of monthly data would be needed for 80% power on a Sharpe-ratio test of TSMOM versus buy-and-hold, and about 90 years for an alpha test — [Zakamulin & Giner 2022, IRFA](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1057521922001363)

#### 1. Trend following / time-series momentum (managed futures, CTAs)
- **Edge source:** behavioural under-reaction and then over-reaction (herding), plus a "crisis alpha" convexity. TSMOM shows a "smile" against equities, doing well in extreme up and down years — [Hurst, Ooi & Pedersen 2017, "A Century of Evidence on Trend-Following Investing", JPM (AQR PDF)](https://www.aqr.com/-/media/AQR/Documents/Insights/Journal-Article/AQR-JPM-Fall-2017.pdf)
- **Long-run evidence:** an equal-weight combination of 1-, 3- and 12-month TSMOM on 67 markets (29 commodities, 11 equity indices, 15 bond markets, 12 currency pairs) from 1880 to 2016. It is positive in every decade and in every market, with an average per-market Sharpe ratio of about 0.4. It made positive returns in 8 of the 10 largest 60/40 drawdowns. Costs were simulated, and 2-and-20 fees applied — [Hurst, Ooi & Pedersen (AQR PDF)](https://www.aqr.com/-/media/AQR/Documents/Insights/Journal-Article/AQR-JPM-Fall-2017.pdf)
- Annualised return of about 11.2% net, with about 9.7% volatility, over 1880 to 2016 — [Alpha Architect summary](https://alphaarchitect.com/portfolio-efficiency) **[secondary. Whether the figure is net of fees or only net of costs should be checked in the paper's Exhibit 1]**
- **Out-of-sample breadth:** applied to 82 new instruments (EM equity futures, swaps, EM FX, exotic commodities, CDS indices, vol futures) and 16 equity factors, 12-month TSMOM has a gross Sharpe of 1.17 on traditional assets, 1.34 on alternative assets and 0.95 on factors, and **1.60 combined**. The median single-asset Sharpe is only about 0.34 to 0.39 — [Babu et al. 2020, "Trends Everywhere", JOIM](https://images.aqr.com/-/media/AQR/Documents/Insights/Journal-Article/AQR-Trends-Everywhere_JOIM.pdf)
- **Critique:** much of the original Moskowitz-Ooi-Pedersen TSMOM alpha comes from **volatility scaling**. Unscaled TSMOM alpha (0.39%/month) is statistically similar to unscaled buy-and-hold; scaled TSMOM gives 1.08%/month, and scaled buy-and-hold gives 0.73%/month — [Kim, Tse & Wald 2016, JFM](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S1386418116301379). AQR replies that the alphas survive after controlling for vol scaling and static bets — [Trends Everywhere](https://images.aqr.com/-/media/AQR/Documents/Insights/Journal-Article/AQR-Trends-Everywhere_JOIM.pdf)
- **Live industry reality (decay or regime):** the SG Trend Index (10 largest CTAs) has a since-inception CAGR of 5.33% with a 20.61% max drawdown. It returned +2.39% in 2025 while the S&P 500 TR returned +17.89% — [Top Traders Unplugged Dec 2025 report](https://www.toptradersunplugged.com/trend-following-performance-report-december-2025). AQR calculates that SG Trend earned 4.0% excess of cash from 2000 to September 2025, a **Sharpe of about 0.30**, with 4.7% alpha to equities (t = 1.8) — [AQR "Hold the Dip" 2025](https://www.aqr.com/-/media/AQR/Documents/Alternative-Thinking/AQR-Alternative-Thinking---Hold-the-Dip.pdf?sc_lang=en)
- SG Trend made +27.3% in 2022 (its best year, beating 2008) and was then hit by trend reversals in 2023 (the SVB banking crisis) — [Hedgeweek Oct 2023](https://www.advancedalphaadvisers.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/11/HW-Trend-Following-The-Outlook-for-CTA-and-Systematic-Investing.pdf). CTAs were the worst-performing hedge fund strategy in H1 2025 and were in a deep 13-month drawdown driven by tariff-induced V-shaped reversals — [With Intelligence 2025](https://www.withintelligence.com/insights/cta-hedge-fund-report). SG Trend was -9.3% YTD to end-April 2025, while the SG Short-Term Traders Index was -0.9% — [SG CTA update May 2025](https://content.sgmarkets.com/CTA_UPDATE_KEEPING_UP_WITH_THE_TRENDFOLLOWERS_2025)
- **Holding period and speed:** in Carver's backtests, moving-average crossovers faster than about MAC8 (8/32 days) have near-zero or negative Sharpe even before costs (MAC2,8 pre-cost SR 0.07) and lose money after costs. The slowest (MAC64,256) has SR about 0.28 per instrument with about 1.1 trades a year. His heuristic "speed limit" is to spend no more than one third of expected pre-cost returns on costs — [Carver, "How fast should we trade?" 2020](https://qoppac.blogspot.com/2020/04/how-fast-should-we-trade.html). In Bitcoin too, slow (12-week baseline) TSMOM signals beat fast ones — [Kang & Ryu 2026, Risk Management](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1057/s41283-026-00234-7)
- **Failure regimes:** sharp V-shaped reversals and choppy, trendless markets. Examples are 2011, 2018 and early 2023 (SVB), and April 2025 (tariffs) — [Man Group 2025](https://www.man.com/insights/deep-dive-trend-following); [With Intelligence](https://www.withintelligence.com/insights/cta-hedge-fund-report)
- **Solo verdict:** this is the best-evidenced family that works on daily bars. It needs many uncorrelated instruments to be worthwhile (see Q3). Single-instrument Sharpe is low (about 0.3 to 0.4). Free daily data for ETFs, FX and crypto is enough. Live Sharpe for large CTAs has been about 0.3 since 2000, far below backtests.

#### 2. Cross-sectional momentum (equities and other asset classes)
- **Evidence:** value and momentum premia are present in 8 markets and asset classes (US, UK, Europe and Japan stocks; country index futures; government bonds; FX; commodities), with a strong common factor structure. Momentum loads positively on funding liquidity risk — [Asness, Moskowitz & Pedersen 2013, JF](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1111/jofi.12021)
- Over a century, the Sharpe ratio of momentum across all asset classes is 0.64. US stock momentum earns 8.2%/year at 15.8% volatility (SR 0.52) from 1926 to 2020, with skew -3.0 — [Ilmanen et al. 2021, "How Do Factor Premia Vary Over Time?", JOIM](https://images.aqr.com/-/media/AQR/Documents/Journal-Articles/JOIM_How-Do-Factor-Premia-Vary-Over-Time.pdf)
- **Crashes:** past losers rose 232% in July and August 1932, and losers rose 163% in March to May 2009 while winners gained 8%. Crashes happen in "panic states" (after a market decline, with high volatility, then a rebound) and are partly forecastable. A dynamic, volatility-scaled momentum strategy roughly doubles the Sharpe (from 0.68 to about 1.20) — [Daniel & Moskowitz 2016, JFE](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0304405X16301490); [Wiest 2022 review](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11408-022-00417-8). UMD lost 43% from March to May 2009, versus -9% for a 50/50 combination with value — [AQR Fact, Fiction 2023](https://www.aqr.com/-/media/AQR/Documents/Journal-Articles/AQRJPMQuant23FactFictionandFactorInvesting.pdf?sc_lang=en)
- **Recent decay:** US momentum earned just 1.2%/year (t = 0.27) from January 2007 to June 2020, against 7.8% for 1963 to 2020 — [Arnott et al. 2021, FAJ (Duke PDF)](https://people.duke.edu/~charvey/Research/Published_Papers/P149_Reports_of_values.pdf)
- **Crypto:** large-cap crypto cross-sectional momentum earned 1.74%/week from 2016 to July 2020, significant only at 10%. It was negative and insignificant after July 2020 and is subject to severe crashes; volatility management helps — [Grobys et al. 2025, FMPM](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11408-025-00474-9)
- **Solo verdict:** viable on daily or weekly data with monthly rebalancing. Survivorship-free free equity data is the hard part. It needs crash protection (vol scaling) and works best combined with value or trend.

#### 3. Mean reversion: short-term reversal, pairs/stat-arb, cointegration
- **Lo-MacKinlay:** contrarian (sell winners, buy losers) weekly profits arise partly from **lead-lag cross-autocorrelation** (large stocks lead small ones), not only from overreaction — [Lo & MacKinlay 1990, RFS](https://academic.oup.com/rfs/article/3/2/175/1595488)
- **Pairs, Gatev-Goetzmann-Rouwenhorst:** distance-method pairs earned annualised excess returns of up to 11% from 1962 to 2002 (about 90 bps a month gross). Net of bid-ask, the estimate falls to 19 to 38 bps a month — [GGR 2006, RFS](https://academic.oup.com/rfs/article-abstract/19/3/797/1646694); cost figures from [Do & Faff 2012, JFR](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1111/j.1475-6803.2012.01317.x)
- **Decay:** the top-20-pairs excess return was 0.86%/month (1962 to 1988), 0.37% (1989 to 2002), and **0.24% (2003 to 2009)**. Pairs trading does well in prolonged turbulence (2000 to 2002, 2007 to 2009). About 70% of the decline is attributed to worsening arbitrage risks (divergence, noise-trader and synchronisation risk) rather than to competition alone — [Do & Faff 2010, FAJ](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/abs/10.2469/faj.v66.n4.1)
- After commissions, market impact and short fees, the average pairs return falls from 93 to 12 bps a month. Pairs formed within refined industries earn about 30 bps a month risk-adjusted. Both pairs and industry reversal are **largely unprofitable after 2002** — [Do & Faff 2012](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1111/j.1475-6803.2012.01317.x)
- Distance, cointegration and copula methods on the US market from 1962 to 2014 earn 91, 85 and 43 bps a month gross, and **38, 33 and 5 bps net** — [Rad, Low & Faff 2016, QF](https://randlow.github.io/2016_QF_PairsTrading.pdf). On 1990 to 2020 data, static rules give about 0.23 to 0.26% a month and fail to beat the market except in bear markets and Covid — [Fil 2020, arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2010.01157v1)
- International evidence: pairs abnormal returns persist across 34 markets, with at least 12% annualised in liquid US stocks, but are highly time-varying — [Jacobs & Weber 2015, JFM](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1386418114000809)
- **Short-term reversal:** conventional reversal is about zero or negative net of costs, but **residual** (factor-adjusted) reversal earns more than 7%/year net and stays positive post-1990 even in the 100 largest stocks — [Blitz et al. 2013, JFM](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S1386418112000468). Restricting to large caps plus turnover-reducing construction gives 30 to 50 bps a week net (US), and up to 20 bps a week in Europe — [de Groot, Huij & Zhou 2012, JBF](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0378426611002263)
- **Practitioner check:** in Carver's futures system, mean-reversion rules have negative standalone Sharpe (mrinasset160 = -0.63), but he keeps them for their negative correlation with trend — [Carver "My trading system" 2021](https://qoppac.blogspot.com/2021/12/my-trading-system.html)
- **Solo verdict:** simple pairs trading on daily bars is close to dead after costs in liquid US equities. Residual reversal is the most promising variant but needs a factor model, a large universe, and cheap execution and shorting. Shorting single stocks is a practical constraint for an Australian retail operator (see Gaps).

#### 4. Carry (FX, futures, crypto funding/basis)
- **Evidence:** carry predicts returns cross-sectionally and in the time series in global equities, bonds, commodities, US Treasuries, credit and options. A long-short carry trade averages **Sharpe 0.8 per asset class and 1.2 diversified across classes**. Carry timing averages SR 0.6, or 0.9 combined. It is exposed to global recession, liquidity and volatility risk — [Koijen, Moskowitz, Pedersen & Vrugt 2018, JFE "Carry"](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0304405X17302908)
- Over a century, carry across all asset classes has SR 0.57 — [Ilmanen et al. 2021](https://images.aqr.com/-/media/AQR/Documents/Journal-Articles/JOIM_How-Do-Factor-Premia-Vary-Over-Time.pdf). In Carver's live futures system, carry rules have backtest SR of about 0.90 to 0.95, the best of any rule: "If you could only trade one rule, I guess it would be carry" — [Carver 2021](https://qoppac.blogspot.com/2021/12/my-trading-system.html)
- **FX carry crash risk:** carry trades "go up by the stairs and down by the elevator". High interest-rate differentials predict negative skewness, and unwinds coincide with VIX spikes — [Brunnermeier, Nagel & Pedersen 2008, NBER Macro Annual](https://www.journals.uchicago.edu/doi/10.1086/593088). Testing 48 currencies over 36 years with data-snooping corrections, carry strategies picked as profitable in one period are generally not profitable in the next. Consistency is confined to 1998 to 2005 — [Out-of-sample performance of carry trades, 2024, JIMF](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0261560624000299). MXN/JPY carry was wiped out in mid-2024 (Mexican election plus a BoJ hike), then recovered from mid-2025 — [Banque de France blog 2026](https://www.banque-france.fr/en/publications-and-statistics/publications/carry-trades-and-volatility-risk)
- **Crypto carry (futures basis):** it averages about 7 to 8% a year and spikes. It exists because of limits to arbitrage: no cross-margining between spot and futures, and regulatory barriers. At 10x leverage, the futures leg would have been liquidated in more than half the months sampled — [Schmeling, Schrimpf & Todorov, VoxEU/CEPR](https://cepr.org/voxeu/columns/crypto-carry-market-segmentation-and-price-distortions-digital-asset-markets). After the 2024 spot BTC ETF launch, average carry fell by about 3 percentage points across exchanges, plus a further 5 points on CME — [BSIC summary of SST](https://bsic.it/carry-trading-dynamics-in-cryptocurrency-markets) **[secondary]**
- **Perp funding-rate carry:** long spot, short perp, and collect funding when funding is positive. It "scales and doesn't need speed". The risks are funding flipping negative, liquidation of the perp leg, and paying two spreads to enter and exit. Funding of 0.05% per 8 hours is about 55% gross annualised, but crowding compresses it quickly — [CCXT blog, July 2026](https://docs.ccxt.com/blog/crypto-arbitrage-strategies) **[practitioner source]**
- CEX-DEX funding arbitrage returns are driven by funding carry but are "highly assumption-sensitive". Counterparty-risk episodes are more damaging than price crashes — [Digital Finance 2026](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s42521-026-00213-3). In cross-venue funding spreads, 17% of observations show spreads of at least 20 bps, but only 40% of top opportunities are profitable after costs and reversals, and forced exits occur in 95% — [Mathematics 2026, "Two-Tiered Structure of Crypto Funding Rate Markets"](https://www.mdpi.com/2227-7390/14/2/346/xml)
- **Solo verdict:** strong evidence and low data needs: funding-rate history is free through exchange APIs and CCXT's fetchFundingRateHistory. Crypto funding and basis carry can be paper-traded on hourly data. Key risks are exchange and counterparty risk (FTX), liquidation, and regime changes in funding levels. Multi-asset futures carry needs futures roll and term-structure data, which is harder to get free.

#### 5. Value and other equity factors (profitability, investment, low-beta/defensive)
- **Value:** HML suffered a 55% drawdown from January 2007 to June 2020, the deepest and longest since 1963 (13.5 years). A bootstrap puts a drawdown that large at about a 2.3% probability. The authors attribute it to intangibles being ignored by book value and to valuation spreads collapsing, **not** to value's death. HML returned -5.4%/year from 2007 to mid-2020, against 3.0%/year over 1963 to 2020 — [Arnott, Harvey, Kalesnik & Linnainmaa 2021, FAJ](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/0015198X.2020.1842704)
- Over the same window, profitability (RMW) held up (3.2%/year, t = 2.08), while investment, size and low beta were flat or negative — [Arnott et al. (Duke PDF), Table 1](https://people.duke.edu/~charvey/Research/Published_Papers/P149_Reports_of_values.pdf)
- "Purified" US value long-short returned +15.7% in 2022 — [Alpha Centauri / iSTOXX 2022 update](https://www.alpha-centauri.com/uploads/image_asset_contentr_download_paragraph_download/file/309/20221230_iSTOXX_Europe_Update.pdf) **[secondary/vendor]**
- **Century view:** Sharpe ratios across all asset classes are value 0.53, momentum 0.64, carry 0.57, defensive 0.68. The **four-factor multi-asset combination has SR 1.46 (t = 14.2)**. Factor Sharpe ratios vary a lot over time — [Ilmanen et al. 2021](https://images.aqr.com/-/media/AQR/Documents/Journal-Articles/JOIM_How-Do-Factor-Premia-Vary-Over-Time.pdf)
- Value and momentum correlate at about -0.64 in AQR's style premia data (1990 to 2013) — [Israel/AQR "Investing With Style" 2015, JOIM](https://www.aqr.com/-/media/AQR/Documents/Insights/Journal-Article/JOIM-Investing-With-Style.pdf)
- **Solo verdict:** valid as slow, monthly-rebalanced long-only tilts or long-short, but this needs point-in-time fundamentals. Free fundamental data with point-in-time accuracy is scarce (see Gaps). Expect decade-long drawdowns. Value is better as a diversifier against momentum than as a standalone strategy.

#### 6. Volatility strategies (selling vol / VRP; vol targeting)
- **VRP selling:** in a Barclays backtest from 1996 to August 2022, a hedged short variance-swap strategy returned 12.26% a year at 3.11% volatility (return/vol 3.94), with a -10.23% max drawdown. A short-put strategy returned 8.06% at 10.84% volatility (0.74), with a -37.09% max drawdown. Note that weekly-option implied vols before 2010 were extrapolated — [Barclays VRP paper 2022](https://indices.cib.barclays/dms/Public%20marketing/Volatility_Risk_Premium.pdf) **[sell-side backtest]**
- The VRP is a "downside, crash-conditional risk premium… not harvestable carry". In a calm year it can mimic a Sharpe above 6, yet even vol-targeted it keeps an irreducible left tail. Selling vol into a jump loses. The premium appears in gold, BTC and ETH (via Deribit DVOL) as well as equities. Built entirely from free data — [Ahmed 2026, SSRN](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=7046559) **[single-author working paper, not peer-reviewed]**
- **Volmageddon:** on 5 February 2018 the VIX jumped 20 points, its largest daily rise since 1987. Leveraged and short volatility ETPs were forced into rebalancing — [BIS Quarterly Review March 2018](https://www.bis.org/publications/equity-market-turbulence-5-february-role-exchange-traded-volatility-products); [FAJ 2021 "Volmageddon and the Failure of Short Volatility Products"](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/0015198X.2021.1913040)
- **Vol targeting / volatility-managed portfolios:** scaling by inverse past variance gives large alphas, for example market alpha of 4.9% and +25% Sharpe — [Moreira & Muir 2017, JF](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1111/jofi.12513). Across 103 strategies, though, volatility-managed portfolios **do not systematically beat** unmanaged ones in real-time out-of-sample tests, because of structural instability — [Cederburg et al. 2020, JFE](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0304405X2030132X). Scaling by downside volatility does better (positive alpha in 89 of 94 anomalies) — [Wang & Yan 2021, JBF](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0378426621001576). Across more than 60 assets, vol targeting raises Sharpe for risk assets (equities, credit), consistently cuts tail risk and max drawdowns, and has negligible effect on bonds, FX and commodities — [Harvey et al. 2018, JPM "The Impact of Volatility Targeting"](https://people.duke.edu/~charvey/Research/Published_Papers/P135_The_impact_of.pdf)
- **Solo verdict:** vol targeting is a portfolio-construction layer, not a source of alpha. It should be on by default in the factory. Short-vol harvesting needs options data and execution: free options data is scarce, and paper fills on options are unreliable. Its crash profile makes it dangerous to promote from backtest. Use it only with defined-risk structures, if at all.

#### 7. Arbitrage (index/ETF, triangular, cross-exchange crypto, funding-rate)
- **ETF arbitrage:** only authorized participants (clearing-agency members with an agreement with the ETF) can create or redeem units. Others can only trade ETF against basket in the secondary market — [SEC Rule 6c-11 release 2019](https://www.sec.gov/files/rules/final/2019/33-10695.pdf)
- **Index futures vs ETF (ASX):** arbitrage frequency and profit on the SPI futures and S&P/ASX200 ETF pair are linked to the number of co-located minimum-latency traders — [Pacific-Basin Finance Journal 2017](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0927538X16301020)
- **Cross-exchange crypto:** price deviations "hardly exist since April 2018" (the end of the Makarov-Schoar sample). It is "barely possible to exploit existing price differences", and informed and institutional trading is linked to the decline — [Crépellière, Pelster & Zeisberger 2023, JFM](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1386418123000150). Settlement latency exposes arbitrageurs to price risk — [Hautsch et al., "Building Trust Takes Time"](https://arxiv.org/html/1812.00595v4). Remaining opportunities persist where the high-price exchange carries higher default risk — [Guo et al. 2024, FRL](https://orca.cardiff.ac.uk/id/eprint/173664/1/1-s2.0-S154461232401393X-main.pdf). The version that works holds inventory on both venues; the naive transfer-based version "rarely survives contact with reality" — [CCXT blog 2026](https://docs.ccxt.com/blog/crypto-arbitrage-strategies) **[practitioner]**
- **Funding-rate arbitrage:** see Carry. It is the only "arbitrage" here that does not need speed — [CCXT blog](https://docs.ccxt.com/blog/crypto-arbitrage-strategies)
- **Solo verdict:** effectively closed except funding/basis carry (see Q2). Paper trading also cannot model queue position or latency, so a paper-traded latency arbitrage would give falsely positive results (inference).

#### 8. Market making
- See Q2. There is no evidence that a retail operator on hourly bars can market-make profitably. Market making is inherently intrabar and depends on queue position.

#### 9. Event-driven: PEAD, index rebalancing, seasonality/calendar
- **PEAD:** large stocks have shown no PEAD since 2006, and it has only recently disappeared in microcaps — [Martineau 2022, Critical Finance Review "Rest in Peace PEAD"](https://www.emerald.com/cfr/article/11/3-4/613/1324916/Rest-in-Peace-Post-Earnings-Announcement-Drift). Papers in 2025 claiming PEAD is alive include microcaps. Subrahmanyam finds t = 2.18 with all stocks but **t = 1.43 excluding microcaps** — [UCLA Anderson Review 2026](https://anderson-review.ucla.edu/is-post-earnings-announcement-drift-a-thing-again). Bernard-Thomas originally found about 2% drift over 60 days (about 18% annualised long-short). The decline is attributed to more arbitrage and a better information environment — [Fink 2021 review, JBEF](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2214635020303750)
- **Index inclusion:** the abnormal return on S&P 500 additions fell from 7.4% in the 1990s to under 1% (0.3% in the working-paper version) in the 2010s. Deletions earned only 0.1% from 2010 to 2020. Similar declines show up in other index families — [Greenwood & Sammon 2025, JF](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1111/jofi.13410); [HBS WP](https://www.hbs.edu/ris/download.aspx?name=23-025.pdf)
- **Turn of month:** the turn-of-month premium was +0.10%/day (t = 3.0) from 1962 to 1980 and similar from 1981 to 2000, and is **completely insignificant from 2001 to 2020**. The decline is linked to decimalisation and lower costs — [Han, Han & Tian 2025, FRL](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1544612324014909)
- **Calendar anomalies generally:** day-of-week, week-of-month and January effects survive data-mining corrections over the full sample but "largely disappear" from the early 1990s. Sell-in-May is the most resilient, with international evidence robust to data-mining correction — [Calendar anomalies: real patterns or data-mining artifacts?, 2026](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1062940826000756)
- **Solo verdict:** mostly decayed in liquid US equities. They may persist in less-efficient markets (ASX small caps, crypto), but that is untested here. They are cheap to test on daily bars, which makes them good candidates for automated mining with strict multiple-testing correction. Expect low hit rates.

#### 10. Breakout / technical patterns
- Sullivan, Timmermann & White apply White's Reality Check to about 8,000 rule parameterisations on 100 years of the DJIA. Some rules beat the benchmark from 1897 to 1986, but there is **no evidence of value from 1987 to 1996** out of sample, and none on S&P 500 futures from 1984 to 1996 — [STW 1999, JF](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1111/0022-1082.00163)
- Using false-discovery-rate control on the DJIA from 1897 to 2011, investors could never have selected the future best rules ex ante, and in-sample performance is "completely offset by the introduction of low transaction costs" — [Bajgrowicz & Scaillet 2012, JFE](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0304405X1200116X)
- Across 23 developed and 18 emerging markets, predictability "diminishes drastically over time". Only 5 developed and 4 emerging markets keep significant rules at 20 bp one-way cost. The best recent rules **underperform buy-and-hold out of sample** — [FMPM 2023](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11408-023-00433-2)
- **Exception:** slow breakout rules used as trend filters across diversified futures work as part of trend following. Carver's breakout40 to breakout320 have SR 0.57 to 0.79, while breakout10 has SR -1.19 — [Carver 2021](https://qoppac.blogspot.com/2021/12/my-trading-system.html)
- **Solo verdict:** pattern rules on single instruments are the prototypical data-mining trap. A factory must apply Reality Check, SPA or FDR corrections to whole families of parameterisations. Slow breakouts are acceptable as trend variants.

#### 11. ML-based prediction
- Gu, Kelly & Xiu study about 30,000 US stocks over 1957 to 2016 with more than 900 baseline signals. Neural networks and trees are best; "shallow" beats "deep" because of the low signal-to-noise ratio. The dominant predictors are price trends (reversal and momentum), then liquidity, volatility and valuation. Decile-spread Sharpe is 2.42 equal-weighted and 1.23 value-weighted (NN ensemble) — [Gu, Kelly & Xiu 2020, RFS](https://academic.oup.com/rfs/article/33/5/2223/5758276)
- Deep-learning profits come from **difficult-to-arbitrage stocks** and high limits-to-arbitrage states. Excluding microcaps or distressed stocks, or high-volatility episodes, "considerably attenuates profitability", and performance "further deteriorates" with reasonable trading costs because of high turnover — [Avramov, Cheng & Metzker 2023, Management Science](https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/10.1287/mnsc.2022.4449)
- Penalising costs in training restores some performance. In non-microcaps, the plain model's out-of-sample R² is 0.03% versus 0.27% for the cost-aware one, and Sharpe is 0.758 versus 1.071 — [Jo, Kim & Shin 2025 WP](https://iksa.or.kr/file/download/9320) **[working paper]**
- ML can help *within* proven families. Nonlinear TSMOM raises the 12-month Sharpe from 0.73 (cross-sectional) to 1.03 — [Moskowitz, Sabbatucci, Tamoni & Uhl, Dec 2025, "Nonlinear Time Series Momentum"](https://afajof.org/management/viewp.php?n=235172). Timing over 300 factors with PLS gives a median gain of about 2% a year; a timed long-short factor portfolio has SR 1.3 versus 0.79 — [Neuhierl et al. 2023 "Timing the factor zoo"](https://afajof.org/management/viewp.php?n=32508)
- **Solo verdict:** ML is a modelling technique, not an edge source. It is highest-risk for overfitting. Its published gains concentrate in untradable microcaps. Use it to combine or condition known signals (trend, carry, reversal), with cost-aware objectives and walk-forward validation.

#### 12. Portfolio-level approaches (risk parity, ensembles of weak signals)
- **Risk parity:** 2021 and 2022 broke its stock-bond diversification assumption. A basic UK risk parity model drew down up to about 30% in 2022, worse than 60/40 — [LGIM blog 2023](https://blog.landg.com/categories/investment-strategy/risk-parity--the-quant-favourite-struggled-through-2022). 2022 was the first year with double-digit losses in both the S&P 500 (-18.1%) and long Treasuries (-26.1%); 60/40 lost 20.3% — [Alpha Architect 2023](https://alphaarchitect.com/stock-bond-correlation). Adding trend following to risk parity improves risk-adjusted returns in backtests — [LongTail Alpha 2025](https://www.longtailalpha.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/09/LongTail-Alpha-Risk-Parity-With-Trend-Following-1.pdf) **[manager white paper]**
- **Ensembles:** see Q3. The combined multi-factor multi-asset portfolio has SR 1.46 against single factors at 0.53 to 0.68 — [Ilmanen et al. 2021](https://images.aqr.com/-/media/AQR/Documents/Journal-Articles/JOIM_How-Do-Factor-Premia-Vary-Over-Time.pdf)

### Inferences
- Ranking by strength of persistent net-of-cost evidence (my synthesis): (1) diversified trend following; (2) multi-asset carry; (3) a value plus momentum plus carry plus defensive combination; (4) residual short-term reversal in large caps; then a large gap to (5) pairs trading, PEAD, calendar and index effects, and single-asset technical rules, which are mostly decayed in liquid markets.
- Families that survive tend to be **risk premia or slow behavioural effects with crash risk** (trend, carry, value, momentum, VRP). Families that died are **pure mispricings easily arbitraged once costs fell** (TOM, index effect, PEAD, simple pairs, cross-exchange crypto). This suggests the factory should weight its search toward explainable premia, and treat newly "discovered" pure-price patterns with heavy scepticism.
- A post-publication haircut of about 50% is a reasonable prior for any strategy the factory finds that resembles a published anomaly. A statistically "discovered" strategy needs an even larger haircut, given the number of trials.
- The gap between live and backtest results is large even for the best family: SG Trend's Sharpe has been about 0.3 since 2000, against backtest Sharpe ratios above 1.

### Gaps
- I did not retrieve Hurst-Ooi-Pedersen's Exhibit 1 numbers directly. The 11.2%/9.7% figure is from Alpha Architect, and whether it is net of 2/20 fees is unverified.
- No sourced live track record of trend or carry implemented on *ETFs only* (the likely retail/Australian implementation, rather than futures).
- No source checked for free point-in-time fundamentals or survivorship-free equity data (needed for value and cross-sectional momentum). This belongs to the data-sources researcher.
- No source found on ASX-specific anomaly persistence (PEAD, reversal, or calendar effects in Australian equities).
- Gatev-Goetzmann-Rouwenhorst's "11%" is an annualised excess return before full costs. A full post-2010 net-of-cost replication was not found beyond Fil 2020 (US 1990 to 2020).

## Key Question 2: Which families are effectively closed to a solo operator (latency-sensitive arbitrage, HFT market making), and why?

### Takeaway
Latency arbitrage, HFT market making, cross-exchange and triangular crypto arbitrage, and index/ETF arbitrage are effectively closed. Winning is decided in microseconds, and race profits concentrate in a handful of firms with co-location and microwave links. ETF creation-redemption is restricted to authorized participants by rule. Hourly-bar paper trading also cannot represent queue position or latency, so such strategies cannot even be validated in this setup. Short-vol and options strategies are open in principle but practically hard on free data and paper fills.

### Cited Findings
- On LSE FTSE 100 stocks, latency-arbitrage races happen about once a minute per symbol (537 a day). The modal race lasts **5 to 10 microseconds** and races account for about 20% of volume. **The top 6 firms win 82% of races** (top 3 win about 55%). Each race is worth about half a tick, adding up to about $5bn a year globally. It acts as a 0.42 to 0.53 bp "tax" on trading — [Aquilina, Budish & O'Neill 2022, QJE](https://academic.oup.com/qje/article/137/1/493/6368348)
- What matters is being *faster than competitors*, not just fast. The fastest HFT firms earn much higher revenues, concentration is persistent and not declining, and new entrants are typically slower, earn less and are more likely to exit — [Baron, Brogaard, Hagströmer & Kirilenko, "Risk and Return in High-Frequency Trading" (Imperial PDF)](https://spiral.imperial.ac.uk/server/api/core/bitstreams/efefa306-435f-471c-9e4e-087ee65fd4af/content). HFTs use microwave transmission and co-location (same source, citing Shkilko & Sokolov 2016 and Brogaard et al. 2015)
- In FX, as more HFTs enter, "the scope to profit purely from the ability to trade with low latency is expected to diminish", and HFT firms are moving into directional strategies — [BIS Markets Committee 2011](https://www.bis.org/publications/high-frequency-trading-foreign-exchange-market.pdf)
- **ASX index arbitrage** (SPI futures vs ASX200 ETF): opportunities increase with the number of minimum-latency co-location connections, which makes this a co-location game — [PBFJ 2017](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0927538X16301020)
- **ETF primary-market arbitrage** is reserved to authorized participants, which must be clearing-agency members with a contract with the ETF — [SEC 2019](https://www.sec.gov/files/rules/final/2019/33-10695.pdf)
- **Cross-exchange crypto arbitrage** has been barely exploitable since April 2018 — [JFM 2023](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1386418123000150). The workable form needs pre-positioned inventory on several exchanges, with exchange default risk — [CCXT 2026](https://docs.ccxt.com/blog/crypto-arbitrage-strategies); [Guo et al. 2024](https://orca.cardiff.ac.uk/id/eprint/173664/1/1-s2.0-S154461232401393X-main.pdf)
- **Open exception:** funding-rate and basis carry "doesn't need speed" — [CCXT 2026](https://docs.ccxt.com/blog/crypto-arbitrage-strategies). Its risks are liquidation and counterparty failure, not latency — [Digital Finance 2026](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s42521-026-00213-3); [VoxEU](https://cepr.org/voxeu/columns/crypto-carry-market-segmentation-and-price-distortions-digital-asset-markets)
- **Small-capital granularity** also matters for futures. With only enough capital for one contract at maximum position, Carver's binary or threshold sizing loses about 20% of Sharpe compared with continuous sizing — [Carver 2016](https://qoppac.blogspot.com/2016/03/diversification-and-small-account-size.html)

### Inferences
- "Closed" families share three traits: (a) the edge is a rank-order race on speed; (b) access depends on membership or infrastructure (AP status, co-location, exchange rebates and tiers); (c) the edge per trade is about one tick, so any extra cost (retail fees, spread) wipes it out. Hourly bars cannot represent any of these.
- Retail market making in crypto (posting passive quotes through an API) is technically possible but faces adverse selection from faster participants. I found no evidence that it is profitable at retail latency. Treat it as closed unless proven otherwise (inference, unsourced).
- The factory should hard-exclude strategies whose backtested edge per trade is below a few spreads, or whose holding period is shorter than the bar size. Such results are fill-model artefacts.
- Partly closed: single-stock short-selling strategies (long-short value, momentum, pairs) depend on borrow availability and cost, which an Australian retail broker may not provide (inference; see Gaps).

### Gaps
- No source on what Australian retail brokers (for example IBKR Australia) offer for short-selling US or ASX stocks, futures access, or crypto perpetuals. ASIC restricts retail crypto derivatives and CFDs (from memory, unverified here). The availability of perpetual futures to Australian retail clients in 2026 should be checked by the regulatory researcher.
- No direct evidence on retail crypto market-making profitability.

## Key Question 3: Which families work best as diversified portfolios rather than single strategies?

### Takeaway
Trend following, carry, and factor styles (value, momentum, defensive) are fundamentally **portfolio strategies**. Single-instrument or single-factor Sharpe ratios are about 0.3 to 0.6, while diversified combinations reach about 1.2 to 1.6 in backtests. The negative value-momentum correlation (about -0.6) and trend's crisis convexity make combinations much better than any component. Trend following benefits from breadth much more than long-only allocation does, with about 20 or more independent bets against about 4. For the factory, this suggests mining for *diversifying* sub-strategies to add to a combined, volatility-targeted book, rather than hunting for a single high-Sharpe strategy.

### Cited Findings
- **Trend breadth:** the median single-asset 12-month TSMOM Sharpe is 0.34 to 0.39. Diversified, it is 1.17 (traditional), 1.34 (alternatives), 0.95 (factors), and **1.60 all combined** — [Babu et al. 2020](https://images.aqr.com/-/media/AQR/Documents/Insights/Journal-Article/AQR-Trends-Everywhere_JOIM.pdf)
- **Carver's rules of thumb** for a carry plus EWMAC futures system, by number of instruments: 1 instrument SR 0.35; 2 → 0.45; 4 → 0.54; 8 (one per asset class) → 0.61; 15 → 0.65; 37 → 0.70. The first instrument from each asset class contributes most. The diversification multiplier tops out at about 2.75 — [Carver 2016](https://qoppac.blogspot.com/2016/03/diversification-and-small-account-size.html)
- **Trend versus long-only diversification:** for a trend rule (EWMAC16,64), adding instruments gives a Sharpe improvement of more than 4.5 times (over 20 independent bets) and is still rising. Long-only gives about a 2x improvement (about 4 to 4.3 independent bets) and plateaus around 50 instruments — [Carver, March 2023](https://qoppac.blogspot.com/2023/03?m=1). A retail trader "probably only need[s] 15 to 30" futures for adequate diversification — [Carver, June 2021](https://qoppac.blogspot.com/2021/06?m=0)
- **Carver's live system:** 146 instruments with a median per-instrument Sharpe of 0.27, and an average correlation between subsystems of only 0.05. Costs are about 1.06% a year. Equal-weighting rules that pass the speed limit beat fitted weights (SR 0.726) — [Carver 2021 trading system](https://qoppac.blogspot.com/2021/12/my-trading-system.html); [Carver 2021 forecast weights](https://qoppac.blogspot.com/2021/05/fit-forecast-weights-by-instrument-by.html). Carver's futures account lost 16.3% in the year to April 2025 (his worst year), against benchmarks around -18%. Only relative-value skew, relative carry and fast relative momentum made money — [Carver April 2025](https://qoppac.blogspot.com/2025/04). His all-in costs were about 0.85 to 0.93% a year, and a simple execution algorithm cut slippage by two thirds (same source)
- **Carry diversification:** Sharpe 0.8 per asset class rises to 1.2 diversified — [Koijen et al. 2018](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0304405X17302908)
- **Style combination:** value, momentum, carry and defensive have Sharpe ratios of 0.53 to 0.68 individually across asset classes; the combination has **SR 1.46** (1920s to 2020) — [Ilmanen et al. 2021](https://images.aqr.com/-/media/AQR/Documents/Journal-Articles/JOIM_How-Do-Factor-Premia-Vary-Over-Time.pdf). Value-momentum correlation is -0.64. Value's alpha rises from 3.55% (t = 1.67) to 9.68% (t = 5.74) once the other styles are controlled for — [AQR "Investing With Style"](https://www.aqr.com/-/media/AQR/Documents/Insights/Journal-Article/JOIM-Investing-With-Style.pdf)
- Value and momentum are negatively correlated within and across asset classes — [Asness, Moskowitz & Pedersen 2013](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1111/jofi.12021). In March to May 2009, UMD lost 43% but a 50/50 UMD plus value mix lost only 9% — [AQR 2023](https://www.aqr.com/-/media/AQR/Documents/Journal-Articles/AQRJPMQuant23FactFictionandFactorInvesting.pdf?sc_lang=en)
- Factor momentum (time-series 1-1, 2-12 and 13-60) combined with Fama-French factors reaches a tangency SR of 1.65, against 1.09 for FF5 alone — [Arnott et al. / AQR "Factor Momentum Everywhere" 2019](https://images.aqr.com/-/media/AQR/Documents/Insights/Journal-Article/Factor-Momentum-Everywhere-JPM-Quant-19.pdf)
- Mean-reversion rules with negative standalone Sharpe still earn a positive weight in a diversified system because they are negatively correlated with trend — [Carver 2021](https://qoppac.blogspot.com/2021/12/my-trading-system.html)
- Pairs trading performs best in bear markets and crises, so it diversifies trend and long-only books — [Do & Faff 2010](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/abs/10.2469/faj.v66.n4.1); [Fil 2020](https://arxiv.org/html/2010.01157v1)
- **Risk parity** alone is fragile when the stock-bond correlation turns positive (as in 2022). Augmenting it with trend helps — [LongTail Alpha 2025](https://www.longtailalpha.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/09/LongTail-Alpha-Risk-Parity-With-Trend-Following-1.pdf); [LGIM 2023](https://blog.landg.com/categories/investment-strategy/risk-parity--the-quant-favourite-struggled-through-2022)
- **Multifactor portfolios still have time-varying risk**: even the diversified portfolio shows strong evidence of changing variance, so return per unit of risk varies over time — [Ilmanen et al. 2021](https://images.aqr.com/-/media/AQR/Documents/Journal-Articles/JOIM_How-Do-Factor-Premia-Vary-Over-Time.pdf)

### Inferences
- Design implication for the factory: evaluate candidate strategies on **marginal contribution to an existing ensemble** (correlation to the book, improvement to portfolio Sharpe, behaviour in crises), not on standalone Sharpe. A standalone-negative but anti-correlated rule can be accepted, as Carver does.
- A robust core is plausible on free daily data: slow trend (several speeds) plus carry (crypto funding/basis, and FX or rate carry if the data is available) across 15 to 30 liquid, uncorrelated instruments (ETFs, FX, crypto), volatility-targeted and roughly equal-weighted. Mined strategies would then act as satellites.
- Equal or near-equal weighting with shrinkage beats optimised weights out of sample in Carver's tests. This suggests the factory's allocator should be simple and robust, not a naive Markowitz optimiser.
- Live CTA results (SG Trend Sharpe about 0.3 since 2000; 2023 and 2025 losses) show that even diversified trend can have multi-year flat periods. A paper-trading evaluation window of months will be statistically uninformative for slow strategies. The factory needs decision rules that account for this (for example, Bayesian or long-horizon evaluation rather than kill-on-drawdown).

### Gaps
- No quantified evidence on how many *ETF-based* instruments a retail operator in Australia can access that are actually uncorrelated (versus the futures universes in Carver's studies).
- No source on the diversification benefit of adding crypto trend or carry to a traditional trend book (correlation of crypto TSMOM with commodity, FX and rates TSMOM).
- No source tested ensembles of *machine-mined* weak signals out of sample. Factor-timing evidence (Neuhierl et al.) covers known factors only.
