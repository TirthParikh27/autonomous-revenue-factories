# Ranked by portfolio odds, the consumer app factory comes first

**No verified case of a fully autonomous revenue loop was found.** The documented unattended experiments made little or nothing: of nine 2026 post-mortems, the eight with a stated figure total $71 in revenue (this report's sum of the figures listed) ([verification list and sources below](#case-studies-with-verifiability-ratings)). What solo developers actually run with Claude Code are human-gated pipelines: the agent researches, builds, generates content and reports, and a person approves spend, publishes, closes sales and owns the accounts. The money that can be checked is small. The median subscription app earns about $72 a month a year after launch ([RevenueCat](https://www.revenuecat.com/state-of-subscription-apps-2026/)), and a 38-extension Chrome portfolio measured $31.03 MRR from Stripe ([DEV](https://dev.to/ktg0215/real-numbers-freemium-chrome-extension-monetization-after-6-months-5hga)). The larger headline numbers come from people selling the method or the story. **Revision 2 of this report ranks the models by portfolio expected value**, at the operator's direction: human gates are accepted, and the question is which factory can eventually make real money when most of the work is done by agents and many cheap attempts are possible. On that basis the recommended first build is the **consumer app factory, run as a test-first portfolio**: about a hundred ideas tested with real paid traffic each quarter, only the survivors built, and store releases paced to protect the developer account. Marketplace micro-tools are a close second and share most of the same test rig. The Apify actor portfolio, which the first revision put first because it carries the least risk, drops to third because its payoff per winner is small. Most of the toolset is already available as one-click Claude connectors, and several tool choices changed in the last eight weeks (Sora API gone, Reddit API closing, Stripe MCP key rules from December). The search had limits: Reddit, X and Hacker News threads were not read directly, so absence of a verified success is a finding about what was found, not proof that none exists.

**How to read the evidence tags.** **V** = read on the vendor's or regulator's own page on 16 November 2026. **S** = read on a third-party page that cites the primary. **U** = unverified, or sources conflict. **E** = an estimate, not a sourced figure. Prices are USD unless marked A$. This is research, not legal, tax or financial advice.

## Ranked by portfolio expected value: consumer apps first

**The lens.** A factory is judged as a portfolio of shots. Six things decide its value: what one shot costs, how fast a shot can be read, how many shots one operator and one set of accounts can take in a year, how often a shot hits, what a hit pays, and whether the shots fail together. The chance of at least one hit in N independent shots with hit rate p is 1 − (1 − p)^N, which is why a low hit rate does not rule a model out, and why anything that makes shots fail together (one account ban, one algorithm update) matters more than the hit rate itself. Every figure in this table is an estimate (E) unless a source is linked. Where a base rate exists it is cited.

| Rank | Model | Cost per shot | Time to read a shot | Shots a year | Hit rate (base rate) | Payoff on a hit | Do shots fail together? | Operator time |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | A. Consumer app portfolio, test-first | About $55 per idea tested, about $1,350 per app shipped | Idea: 3 to 10 days of paid traffic. App: 2 to 3 weeks of paid trials | 200 to 400 ideas tested, 8 to 12 apps shipped on one account | 17.3% of subscription apps reach $1K a month within two years, 4.6% reach $10K ([RevenueCat](https://www.revenuecat.com/state-of-subscription-apps-2026/)) | $12K to $120K+ a year in revenue, with an open-ended tail | Yes, if the developer account is lost. Controlled by release cadence and distinctness | 12 to 15 hours per 100 ideas |
| 2 | G. Marketplace micro-tools and micro-SaaS | About $250 per idea tested on the same fake-door rig, about $100 to build | 1 to 2 weeks for the test. Months for organic store traffic | 20 to 40 shipped. Web products have no store cadence limit | 57.1% of 112 tracked extensions earn nothing. Median verified MRR about $136, top decile above $30K ([BigIdeasDB](https://bigideasdb.com/micro-saas-trends-2026), S) | Recurring B2B revenue. Long tail, slower to arrive | Weakly. | 4 | D. Programmatic SEO and directories | $2 to $250 per site | 4 to 9 months | 10 to 20, but each read is slow | No payment-verified directory found. Honest build logs report zero indexing | Low to moderate ad or lead income | Yes. One spam or core update moves every site, and a network of sites is itself a named abuse pattern | Low |

| 7 | C. Algorithmic trading | Near zero per backtest | Months of paper trading | Unlimited | Expected sign negative on the base rates | Scales with capital at risk, not with shots | Portfolio logic inverts: more strategies tested means more false positives, not more winners | Low |

**Why A comes first.** It has the best combination of a measurable shot, a fast read and an open-ended payoff. Paid traffic turns an idea into a number within a week, and the base rate per shipped app is the best-documented in this report. At the dataset's rates and assuming independence, the chance that at least one shipped app reaches $1K a month is 53% with 4 apps, 85% with 10 and 98% with 20. For $10K a month it is 17%, 38% and 61%.

**What the arithmetic does not show.** Those thresholds are revenue, not profit. The median paid cohort returns 34% to 75% of its ad cost in the first week ([Adapty](https://adapty.io/meta-ads-benchmarks/)), so an app can reach $1K a month while losing money. The RevenueCat dataset includes funded teams. Its hit rates fell year on year. Nothing found shows that testing ideas first raises the hit rate, although that is the reason for the design. And no verified solo portfolio at meaningful revenue was found, so this ranking is a judgement about odds, not a report of a proven result.

**The correction to "ship 100 apps".** A hundred ideas can be tested in a quarter. A hundred apps cannot safely be shipped from one developer account in a year. Apple's guideline 4.3 and Google Play's Repetitive Content policy treat many similar apps as spam, enforcement reads submission cadence and listing similarity, and a termination removes every app on the account (details in the consumer app section below). So the factory puts its volume before the store: roughly 100 ideas in, 4 apps out per cycle, 8 to 12 apps a year, each one distinct.

**Why G is a close second.** It uses the same landing-page test, has no store cadence limit for web products, and its winners are recurring B2B revenue. It ranks below A because paid consumer traffic gives a faster and cleaner read than organic store search, and because the verified numbers for small tools are lower. Building A's test rig first gives G most of its pipeline.

**Why F drops to third.** It still has the cheapest shots and the highest chance of some revenue, which is why the first revision ranked it first. But a winning actor pays tens to hundreds of dollars a month, so even a good portfolio is small. It remains the lowest-risk way to prove the shared run wrapper works.

### One cycle of the app factory, costed

## Demand-testing services: real traffic passes ideas, synthetic users only rank them

The operator asked whether services that test demand with AI can be wired into the factories. Three kinds exist. They differ in what they measure, and only one of them measures behaviour.

| Kind | What it measures | Examples and price | Agent access | Verdict for the factory |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Synthetic users and AI persona panels | What a language model says a person would say | Synthetic Users from $12,500 a year ([pricing](https://pricing.syntheticusers.com/), V), with a Claude connector and API ([docs](https://www.syntheticusers.com/connectors), V). Minds $0.12 per response, Ask Rally from $20 a month, Articos $79 a month (S: [Candor comparison](https://www.runcandor.com/journal/best-synthetic-user-research-tools-2026)). Aaru, Simile and Evidenza are enterprise-priced. Sources conflict on Artificial Societies' price | Synthetic Users: official connector. Others: unverified | Do not buy one. Use a plain in-house LLM ranker as a free first filter that can only kill ideas |
| Keyword and store-search data | Whether people search for the problem | Astro $108 a year, Apple Search Ads popularity, Mac only, local MCP in beta ([Astro](https://tryastro.app/), V). Appfigures free Starter, keywords from $44.99 a month, hosted MCP. MobileAction from $15 a month. AppTweak $99 a month (S: [Keyword Race](https://keywordrace.com/best-aso-tools-for-indie-developers), [AsoBeast](https://asobeast.com/best-aso-tools)) | Appfigures and AppTweak: hosted MCP. Whether AppTweak's MCP needs an API plan is unverified, vendor and third parties disagree | Use at idea mining. Search volume shows a problem exists, not that anyone will pay |
| Real-traffic smoke tests | What strangers do when shown an ad and a price | A static page on Cloudflare Pages plus Meta ads is enough. Prelaunch.com adds reservation deposits from $279 a month (S: [The Rundown](https://www.therundown.ai/tools/prelaunch)) and is built mainly for physical products. Waitlist tools report a median 11% visitor-to-signup rate on their own customers ([Waitlister](https://waitlister.me/growth-hub/blog/waitlist-and-product-launch-statistics), vendor data) against 6.6% for landing pages generally | Meta Ads MCP creates campaigns paused. A person activates | This is the gate. Two tranches of real paid traffic before any code is written |

**How well do synthetic signals predict real conversion?** Not well enough to pass an idea, and the evidence is specific about where they fail.

- **They can rank concepts like a survey panel does.** A study with Colgate-Palmolive reached 90% of human test-retest reliability on concept ranking across 57 surveys and 9,300 respondents ([arXiv 2510.08338](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.08338)). The benchmark was stated purchase intent in surveys, in personal care, a category language models know well. It was not purchases, and the authors frame it as a first screen before human panels.
- **They are unreliable on price.** Against real incentive-compatible willingness to pay, synthetic respondents overstated it by 86% and 37% for two products and understated it by 27% for another, with profit estimates off by up to 647% ([Journal of Marketing Analytics, September 2026](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1057/s41270-026-00542-7)).
- **They are over-positive and flatten differences.** Synthetic respondents overestimated positive attitudes and showed less variation than real ones ([NIM Marketing Intelligence Review, 2026](https://www.nim.org/fileadmin/PUBLIC/12_NIM_MIR_Issues/MIR_KI_in_der_Mafo/MIR_AI_Market_Research/Kaiser_ea_AI_MR_Vol_18_No_1_ENG.pdf)). Averages can match while subgroups are 10 to 30 points off ([arXiv 2609.13148](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.13148v1)).
- **Personas can make prediction worse.** In the one study found that scored predictions against real click behaviour (thousands of real headline A/B tests), a plain LLM asked how likely a typical reader was to click picked the real winner 49% of the time and a ten-persona panel 35%, with rank correlations of 0.36 and 0.08 ([arXiv 2609.25010](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.25010)). Both are modest, and the persona version is close to useless.
- **Vendor accuracy claims are the vendors' own.** Synthetic Users cites "85 to 92% parity" on its pricing page. No independent test of any self-serve persona tool against real app conversion was found.

**Real-traffic tests have limits too.** An email signup is weaker than a payment. A reservation deposit is stronger but, in one vendor's own help pages, still a refundable 1% to 5% ([Prelaunch](https://help.prelaunch.com/en/articles/8263057-what-is-a-reservation-deposit)). At the budgets used here a test reads a few hundred visitors, which separates a 0.5% idea from a 3% idea but not a 2% idea from a 3% one. No published dataset links fake-door conversion to later paid conversion for apps, so the pass bar is a rule of thumb. Store-native options do not help before a build: Apple pre-orders need the app submitted and reviewed first ([Apple](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/pre-orders), V) and Google Play pre-registration needs an uploaded app bundle ([Google](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9859047?hl=en), V).

**How this is wired into the app factory.** Idea mining records keyword popularity. A new `synthetic-screen` stage ranks each batch three times with a plain LLM ranker, no personas and no price questions, and a script drops the bottom half. It can kill an idea and can never pass one. The factory then tracks how well that ranking predicted the real click test, so the screen can be removed if it predicts nothing. Passing requires real traffic: a $30 ad click test, then a fake door that shows the price. The page says the app is in development and that nothing is charged, which costs some conversion and keeps the test honest under consumer law and ad policy. The same test stage can be reused by the micro-tools factory.

Three points decided this lowest-risk order. First, the autonomous-company experiments failed on demand and distribution, not on building, so models where a marketplace already supplies buyers fit a headless loop best ([Autocomp](https://dev.to/autocomp/39-days-of-an-autonomous-ai-company-487m-tokens-1117-of-model-spend-0-in-revenue-2dof), [Automaton](https://automatonagency.com/insights/autonomous-agent-revenue-experiment-teardown)). Second, the two models that need the least capital and carry the least account risk come first, because the realistic outcome for any of them is tens to low hundreds of dollars a month. Third, the conditional MCP/x402 option is best run as a rider on the Apify factory: Apify already lets agents pay for an Actor with a prepaid token and no account ([Apify docs](https://docs.apify.com/actors/publishing/monetize)), so the same asset serves both human and agent buyers without separate payment plumbing.

## Environment audit: strong on video and ops, empty on everything else

All facts in this section were observed directly on 16 November 2026 and are verified: local facts by a read-only inventory on the operator's Mac, account facts from the Claude connector list, and cloud facts from the cloud session container.

**Already in place.** The Mac runs Claude Code 2.1.318 on macOS 26.7.3 with node 24, pnpm, uv, git, an authenticated `gh` (scopes gist, read:org, repo, workflow), a running Docker daemon, ffmpeg, jq, sqlite3 and the HeyGen CLI with credentials. The installed skills are dominated by video: a nine-part HyperFrames suite plus skills for faceless explainers, product-launch videos, slideshows and captions. Connected MCP servers are the claude.ai connectors (Claude Docs, HubSpot, Avoma, Notion, Linear, Google Drive, Gmail, Google Calendar), the PostHog and Slack plugins, and a local `node_repl`. The Claude account also has GitHub connected. The cloud session container has claude, node 22, python3, gh, git, uv, bun, pnpm, ffmpeg, docker and Chromium with Playwright preinstalled.

**Missing.** No scraping, search, browser, payments, deploy, database or media MCP server is installed locally. The repository has no `.mcp.json` and held only a README and a LICENSE at audit time. The cloud container has no MCP servers configured. Not on the Mac's PATH: bun, playwright, remotion, supabase, vercel, wrangler, stripe, apify, firecrawl, agent-browser, codex, hyperframes, openbb, shopify, eas, expo, fastlane, duckdb, railway, flyctl, sentry-cli and ngrok. Config folders exist for hyperframes, openbb, agent-browser and codex, but none of those CLIs is on PATH, so they are invoked another way or were removed. This corrects the earlier infrastructure note, which assumed the Supabase CLI, agent-browser and Conductor were installed.

**Constraints that shape the design.** There is no Xcode, so local iOS builds are impossible and the app factory would depend on EAS cloud builds. System Python is 3.9, with uv available for newer versions. The Mac is work-managed with Aikido device protection active, so package installs may be blocked or scanned. That makes cloud scheduled tasks and GitHub Actions the safer default venue, with the Mac reserved for HeyGen and HyperFrames rendering.

## The eight factories in brief

### Apify Store actor portfolio (third on portfolio value, lowest risk)

**Evidence.** Apify keeps 20% and pays creators 80% of fees minus platform usage costs ([Apify terms](https://docs.apify.com/legal/store-publishing-terms-and-conditions), V). It publishes a monthly payout total of $1.7M ([Apify](https://apify.com/partners/actor-developers)). The one small-operator ledger found shows dollars a day ([DEV](https://dev.to/sessionzero_ai/2000-runs-in-3-days-real-revenue-data-from-launching-13-korean-scrapers-on-apify-211h)). The realistic first-quarter expectation of tens to low hundreds of dollars a month is the researchers' extrapolation from that single data point. **Workflow.** The proposed loop (a design, not an observed practice) is: a weekly scan for Store queries with weak incumbents, a compliance gate on each new target site, build from an Apify template, a cost-per-result test, pay-per-event pricing set above measured cost, publish, daily failure monitoring, and monthly pruning. The read examples price at $0.002–$0.005 per result ([DEV](https://dev.to/agenthustler/the-apify-actor-survival-guide-why-99-of-scrapers-get-zero-users-and-how-to-fix-it-5eoh)). **Key tools.** Apify CLI and hosted MCP, Claude Code on a schedule, Playwright. **Failure modes.** Most actors get 0–5 users; under-pricing produces negative payout; challenge winners ran 40 to several hundred Actors with fast issue response, so maintenance is ongoing ([Apify](https://blog.apify.com/apify-builder-spotlight-john-cole)). **Compliance.** Scoped API tokens cannot create or modify Actors, and scheduled runs get an unscoped token ([Apify](https://docs.apify.com/integrations/api)), so the publishing credential needs care. Payout needs identity verification, and balances under the minimum are forfeited after 12 months. Avoid actors for X, Reddit or personal data.

### Marketplace micro-tools (close second)

**Evidence.** An analysis of TrustMRR data puts median verified MRR at about $136 and the AI-tagged median near $7, with a top decile above $30K ([BigIdeasDB](https://bigideasdb.com/micro-saas-trends-2026), S). A 60,000-user extension shows no active subscriptions ([TrustMRR](https://trustmrr.com/startup/60-000-weekly-users-40-000-daily-users-chrome-extension-3-years-old-no-maintenance?metric=mrr)). No verified agent-run portfolio was found. **Workflow.** Proposed: mine incumbent reviews for paid niches, validate with a payment link before building, ship with a paywall from day one, and verify revenue on the processor. **Key tools.** Cloudflare, Stripe or Polar, Playwright, PostHog. **Failure modes.** Shipping volume did not produce revenue in the 38-extension case. **Compliance.** Chrome Web Store has had no built-in payments since 2021, so billing is off-store ([Google](https://developer.chrome.com/docs/webstore/cws-payments-deprecation)). Shopify takes 0% on the first $1M but since 10 November 2026 allows one new app in review at a time and requires 10 paid installs per published app before the next ([Shopify](https://shopify.dev/changelog/posts/shopify-app-store-submission-requirements-build-grow-then-expand)), which rules out a Shopify-first portfolio. Under Australian Consumer Law a "no refunds" term cannot exclude consumer guarantees ([ACCC](https://www.accc.gov.au/business/selling-products-and-services/selling-online)).

### A. Consumer app factory (build first)

**Evidence.** No portfolio case is independently verified. The neutral dataset says new subscription apps per month rose from about 2,000 in January 2022 to 14,700+ in January 2026 and the odds of reaching $1K fell year on year ([RevenueCat](https://www.revenuecat.com/state-of-subscription-apps-2026/)). **Workflow as practised.** Keyword-gap research with ASO tools, a hard paywall (10.7% download-to-paid by day 35 against 2.1% for freemium, same source), AI-generated TikTok slideshows scheduled as drafts through Postiz, and a person adding sound and publishing, about a minute per post ([Larry case](https://x.com/oliverhenry/status/2022011925903667547)). One vendor-authored pipeline automates the publish step by driving a phone over ADB ([Postiz](https://postiz.com/blog/automate-tiktok-slideshow-marketing-ai-agents-postiz)), which TikTok's ban on automation tools "designed to bypass our systems" puts at risk ([TikTok](https://www.tiktok.com/safety/en/policies-and-engagement/integrity-authenticity)). **Key tools.** Expo EAS, RevenueCat, Appfigures, Postiz, HyperFrames. **Failure modes.** Median outcomes, burnout at 30+ apps, and views that do not become revenue. **Compliance.** Apple's 4.3(a) forbids multiple bundle IDs of the same app and 4.3(b) rejects apps "indistinguishable from what's already widely available"; repeated low-effort submissions can end Developer Program membership ([Apple](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines), V). No Apple rule names AI-built apps ([Newly](https://newly.app/guides/guideline-4-3-spam-rejection), S). TikTok restricts unaudited API clients to private posts and names a tool for uploading to your own accounts as not acceptable ([TikTok](https://developers.tiktok.com/docs/en/content-sharing-guidelines), V). For Australia, Google Play's fee is reported as a 10% service fee plus a 5% billing fee on the first $1M from 30 September 2026 (S: blogs quoting Google's [service-fee page](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/14151465), not read on Google's page). **Account risk and the portfolio.** A Google Play termination removes every app and extends to related and new accounts, which Google links by payment details, device, address and network (S: [App Winners](https://appwinners.eu/guides/google-play-account-terminated-what-to-do)). Apple's 2025 transparency report lists 418 app removals citing 4.3 Spam and 90,608 citing fraud under DPLA 3.2(f) ([Apple](https://www.apple.com/legal/app-store/transparency/2025), V). In June 2026 a developer who mass-created AI prototype apps received a Pending Termination Notice under 3.2(f), reversed on appeal after about six weeks (self-reported: [note.com](https://note.com/light_hare2401/n/n1156750784e5?hl=en)). In August 2026 twelve updates submitted within 90 minutes from one API key were all rejected under 4.3(a), with release notes 46% similar ([Apple developer forums](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/842971)). So enforcement reads cadence and similarity. The design implication is a portfolio of distinct apps released slowly: volume at the idea-test stage, one or two new apps a month at most, per-app listings and feature code, an immediate freeze on any spam or fraud citation, and never a second account as a workaround.

### D. Programmatic SEO and directory factory

**Evidence.** No payment-verified directory was found. Honest 2026 build logs report zero: 8,653 URLs submitted and none indexed ([DEV](https://dev.to/_9848c5582063b42abecb7/i-built-8000-programmatic-seo-pages-google-indexed-zero-a-gsc-postmortem-569i)). **Workflow as practised.** Pull place data, clean hard, verify each business against its own site, generate a static site, deploy on a free tier. The one documented pipeline kept 725 verified businesses from 71,000 raw rows in four days for under $250 ([episode summary](https://videohighlight.com/v/I_wbc5ND79o)). **Key tools.** Outscraper or DataForSEO, Crawl4AI, Astro on Cloudflare Pages, Search Console API, Dirstarter at $150–$200 one-time ([Dirstarter](https://dirstarter.com)). **Failure modes.** Near-duplicate pages, an LLM step that silently fell back to templates ([DEV](https://dev.to/morinaga/i-built-3-programmatic-seo-sites-for-25month-using-claude-haiku-heres-the-full-architecture-3pl8)), and position-1 click-through on AI Overview keywords falling from 7.3% to 1.6% ([PPC Land on Ahrefs](https://ppc.land/googles-ai-summaries-now-swallow-58-of-clicks-that-once-went-to-websites/)). **Compliance.** Google's policy lists "scraping feeds, search results, or other content to generate many pages" and "creating multiple sites with the intent of hiding the scaled nature of the content" as abuse ([Google](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/essentials/spam-policies), V). Google ran four spam updates and three core updates in 2026 to date, and a core update that began on 10 November is still rolling out ([status dashboard](https://status.search.google.com/products/rGHU1u87FJnkP6W2GwMi/history), V), so traffic readings this fortnight are unreliable. Ad thresholds: Journey 1,000 sessions, Mediavine $5,000 annual ad revenue ([Mediavine](https://www.mediavine.com/application-requirements/)), Raptive 25,000 pageviews ([Raptive](https://help.raptive.com/hc/en-us/articles/360032840891)). Build one data-rich site, not a network.

### E. B2B outbound and micro-tool factory

**Evidence.** No verifiable revenue case exists for the per-lead demo model at solo scale. The large-sample benchmark is a 3.43% average reply rate, with the top decile at 10.7%+ ([Instantly](https://instantly.ai/cold-email-benchmark-report-2026)). **Workflow as practised.** Google Places data, one templated page rendered per lead (838 in one write-up that reports no outcomes, [WebVegas](https://justinharris.ai/work/webvegas-cold-email/)), a short email, then a human call. Practitioners keep paid data sources and move reasoning into Claude Code ([Wolfe](https://blog.getaob.com/p/can-claude-code-replace-clay-whats)). **Key tools.** Instantly, Clay from $185/month ([Clay](https://www.clay.com/pricing)), PredictLeads, Cal.com, Stripe. Explorium's AgentSource is discontinued on 2 December 2026 ([Explorium](https://www.explorium.ai/pricing/)). **Failure modes.** Campaigns that returned 200 OK with no sender attached, and over $1,400 lost to a proxy priced at twice the direct API (Wolfe, same source). **Compliance, Australia.** This is the hardest constraint in the report. Consent cannot be inferred from mere publication of an address. The narrow exception needs a conspicuously published work address for a particular person or role, no "no marketing" statement, and a message relevant to that role ([Spam Act, Schedule 2](https://classic.austlii.edu.au/au/legis/cth/consol_act/sa200366/sch2.html), S: AustLII copy). The sender bears the evidential burden ([s 16](https://classic.austlii.edu.au/au/legis/cth/consol_act/sa200366/s16.html)). An Australian sender emailing only overseas recipients is still covered ([s 7](https://classic.austlii.edu.au/au/legis/cth/consol_act/sa200366/s7.html)). Lists made with address-harvesting software are banned outright ([ACMA](https://www.acma.gov.au/avoid-sending-spam), V). No small B2B cold emailer appears in ACMA's 2024–2026 register, but one penalty of $500,800 was for 802 messages ([ACMA](https://www.acma.gov.au/investigations-spam-and-telemarketing), V). A lawful pipeline stores proof per send, and needs legal advice before launch.

### C. Self-improving algorithmic trading factory

**Evidence.** No verifiable live track record of a solo developer's LLM strategy loop was found. Over 2004–2024 buy-and-hold significantly outperformed the LLM agents tested ([arXiv 2505.07078](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2505.07078v4)). Of persistent day traders in the best long-run dataset, 97% lost money ([Quantpedia](https://quantpedia.com/retail-day-trading-is-an-uphill-battle/)). **Workflow as practised.** Generate, backtest, gate, paper-trade, escalate capital by hand. One published gate set requires a probability of backtest overfitting below 0.3, out-of-sample Sharpe at least 80% of in-sample, and 30 days of paper trading ([AI Fin Hub](https://aifinhub.pages.dev/articles/backtest-to-paper-to-live-playbook/)); these are one source's rules of thumb. **Key tools.** Alpaca paper trading, IBKR's official MCP, local Python backtesters, Massive or Tiingo data. VectorBT PRO's licence is personal and non-commercial ([VectorBT](https://vectorbt.pro/)). **Failure modes.** Fourteen of 20 generated strategies were one idea ([DEV](https://dev.to/whetlan/i-asked-an-llm-to-generate-20-trading-strategies-14-were-the-same-thing-2f36)); every discarded trial must be logged or the overfitting statistics mean nothing. **Compliance, Australia.** Kalshi names Australia as a restricted jurisdiction ([Kalshi](https://kalshi.com/docs/kalshi-member-agreement.pdf), V). ACMA has blocked Polymarket ([ACMA](https://www.acma.gov.au/blocked-gambling-websites), V). ASIC said on 30 October 2026 that prediction market contracts "have all the features of a bet" ([ASIC](https://www.asic.gov.au/about-asic/news-centre/find-a-media-release/2026-releases/26-261mr-asic-statement-prediction-markets), V). The US $25,000 pattern-day-trader minimum ended on 4 June 2026 ([FINRA](https://www.finra.org/rules-guidance/notices/26-10), V). A systematic, documented operation looks like a business on the ATO's factors, which makes gains ordinary income ([ATO](https://www.ato.gov.au/individuals-and-families/investments-and-assets/capital-gains-tax/shares-and-similar-investments/share-investing-versus-share-trading), V). Treat this factory as a cheap research harness with no revenue expectation.

### B. Autonomous e-commerce factory

**Evidence.** No verified profitable agent-operated store was found; the documented experiments lost money or made nothing. Average net margin among 5,000+ instrumented stores is 19% for dropshipping and print on demand, a survivorship-biased figure ([TrueProfit](https://trueprofit.io/blog/dropshipping-success-rate)). **Workflow as practised.** Ad-library research, Shopify build through the CLI, AI creative, campaign drafts that a person activates. **Key tools.** Shopify AI Toolkit, Meta Ads MCP, FastMoss from $16/month ([FastMoss](https://developers.fastmoss.com/)), Printful and Printify by REST API (no official MCP, S). **Failure modes.** Demand, ad-account standing and cash flow, which are the steps agents do not control. A widely shared account of a permanent Meta ban after Claude Code was given write access is second-hand and unverified ([Porter Metrics](https://portermetrics.com/?p=189199)). **Compliance.** US duty-free de minimis entry ended for all countries on 29 August 2025 and CBP made the suspension indefinite on 24 June 2026 ([Federal Register](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/06/24/2026-12670/indefinite-suspension-of-the-de-minimis-exemption-for-merchandise-arriving-through-all-modes-other), V). Fake or AI-invented testimonials carry up to $53,088 per violation in the US ([FTC template](https://www.ftc.gov/system/files/ftc_gov/pdf/2025-Fake-Review-Warning-Template.pdf), V) and are unlawful in Australia ([ACCC](https://www.accc.gov.au/business/advertising-and-promotions/online-reviews-for-product-and-services), V). Etsy prohibits bot-generated or mass-automated listings ([Etsy](https://www.etsy.com/seller-handbook/article/1522733284810), V). For print on demand the seller owes the refund for a misprint and recovers from the printer afterwards ([ACCC](https://www.accc.gov.au/business/small-business/small-business-toolkit/rights-and-responsibilities-of-your-small-business/small-business-rights-and-responsibilities)). GST registration is required at A$75,000 of turnover ([ATO](https://www.ato.gov.au/businesses-and-organisations/gst-excise-and-indirect-taxes/gst/registering-for-gst), V). The only variant worth keeping is digital products, which folds into build 2.

### Conditional: paid MCP server or x402 endpoint

**Evidence.** An author with a declared commercial interest estimates that 95%+ of MCP servers earn nothing ([DEV](https://dev.to/nicolas_fainstein/the-state-of-mcp-monetization-in-2026-28i4)). Independent analyses put genuine x402 agent commerce at roughly $5K–$11K a month in total, against headline volumes of $43M–$54M settled (S: the verification pass attributes this to agenteconomy.to and TRM Labs via a third party and recorded no link, so treat it as a lead). **Recommendation.** Do not build payment plumbing. Publish tools as Apify actors, and add a direct paid endpoint only for something that shows repeat agent traffic. The official MCP Registry is still in preview and does not scan servers for malware ([MCP Registry](https://modelcontextprotocol.io/registry/about), V).

### Etsy and Fiverr, checked 16 Nov 2026: neither becomes a new factory

**Verdict.** Etsy is a possible sales channel for factory B's digital products, with a person approving every listing, ranked about level with F. It is not a factory of its own. Fiverr is rejected: it is a services job, not a portfolio.

**Evidence beyond the hype.** A 596-point evidence sweep found no documented case of an Etsy shop with no audience, no public persona and no ongoing manual work that kept selling. The opposite result also appears: six listings in a crowded niche made no sales in 11 days ([SellerAIReady](https://www.selleraiready.com/2026/08/selling-printables-to-etsy-sellers-2026.html), E). For Fiverr, no case was found of an AI-run gig portfolio, and the company's own figures show the low-value work AI can do disappearing.

**What this changes.**
- **Etsy.** Factory B keeps marketplace listings out of scope. If you want to try it, enable it through `F-SCOPE-1` as an extra sales channel for B's digital products: agents research demand and draft each design and listing, and you approve, prompt and publish each one by hand. That approval is what Etsy's rules require anyway. Start with one shop of about 20 to 50 listings in one niche. Read the result from Etsy payout data after 60 days and stop if there are no sales. Physical print on demand comes later, as in B, through a domestic printer. Not allowed: patterns, recipes or guides, prompt bundles, trademarks, and listing photos that hide AI use. The upside is that Etsy brings its own buyers, which factories A and G have to pay for. The weakness is small, slow payoffs that all ride on one shop.
- **Fiverr.** Do not build it. Under the portfolio lens, AI-assisted gig work is consulting: each sale needs a person to deliver, the account is the only asset, and the buyers it suits are leaving.

## Conclusion

Two questions give two different answers, and the operator chose the second. "Which factory is safest and best evidenced?" points to marketplaces that bring their own buyers, and to Apify first. "Which factory gives the best odds of one real payoff, if agents make each attempt cheap and a person stays in the loop?" points to consumer apps, because a shot can be priced and read within days, the base rate per shipped app is known, and the payoff is open-ended. At about $1,350 per shipped app and a 17.3% base rate of reaching $1K a month, ten shipped apps give roughly an 85% chance of at least one. That is an argument from published rates, not from a proven solo case, and revenue at that level can still be unprofitable.

The portfolio only works under two conditions, and both are design problems. Shots must be cheap and readable, so volume sits in landing-page tests with real paid traffic, where an idea costs tens of dollars, and synthetic users are used only to decide which ideas deserve that traffic. Shots must not fail together, so the developer account is treated as the scarce asset: distinct apps, a slow release cadence, per-app listings, and a freeze at the first spam or fraud citation.

The earlier findings still hold. Nothing found runs itself and earns. Identity, payments, store submission, ad activation and publishing stay with the operator, revenue is read from the processor, and spend moves only in approved tranches. The existing video skills are a real asset for ad creative and for organic content once an app has earned it, in formats that do not present invented customers.
